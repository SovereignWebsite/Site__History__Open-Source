Hostility and adoption

2001 to 2008.

Microsoft's leaders attacked open source and the GPL in public in 2001123, advertised against Linux4 and claimed patent infringement5; SCO sued IBM over Linux6. In the same years Firefox, Git, Android and GitHub appeared78910, Sun put Java under the GPL11 and the GPL reached version 312.

3 May 2001 Hostility and adoption

On 3 May 2001 Craig Mundie of Microsoft told an audience at New York University's Stern School of Business that the "viral" GPL threatened the intellectual property of any organisation using it.

1 June 2001 Hostility and adoption

Steve Ballmer, Microsoft's chief executive, told the Chicago Sun-Times on 1 June 2001 that Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches.

"Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches,"

Secondary source: reporting Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer', The Register, by Thomas C. Greene, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

The words were said in an interview with the Chicago Sun-Times published on 1 June 2001. That article could not be read: the newspaper's old address returns 404 and the Internet Archive was unreachable from this machine. The quote is taken from The Register's report of 2 June 2001, which quotes the interview. No transcript is known, so the newspaper is the record of the words and The Register is a second-hand record.

Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview

1 June 2001 Hostility and adoption

Ballmer's explanation of the remark was about licensing: he said that using any open source software obliges a company to make the rest of its software open source.

"The way the license is written, if you use any open-source software, you have to make the rest of your software open source,"

Secondary source: reporting Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer', The Register, by Thomas C. Greene, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

As quoted by The Register from the Sun-Times interview. The Register's reporter argues in the same article that this misreads the GPL.

Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft, GNU General Public License, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview

2005 Hostility and adoption

Git was created in 2005 by Linus Torvalds and the Linux kernel community after they lost free use of the proprietary BitKeeper tool.

In 2005, the relationship between the community that developed the Linux kernel and the commercial company that developed BitKeeper broke down, and the tool’s free-of-charge status was revoked. This prompted the Linux development community (and in particular Linus Torvalds, the creator of Linux) to develop their own tool

Secondary source: book Pro Git, 1.2 Getting Started: A Short History of Git, git-scm.com (Apress), by Scott Chacon and Ben Straub. Read 27 November 2026.

From the Pro Git book hosted on the Git project's site. Torvalds's own 2005 messages on lore.kernel.org were behind a bot challenge and could not be read.

Connects: Linus Torvalds, Git

2007 Hostility and adoption

Stallman's position is that free software is a movement for users' freedom while open source values mainly practical advantage, and that the GNU Project therefore does not use the term "open source".

The free software movement campaigns for freedom for the users of computing; it is a movement for freedom and justice. By contrast, the open source idea values mainly practical advantage and does not campaign for principles. This is why we do not agree with open source, and do not use that term.

Primary source: own-post Why Open Source Misses the Point of Free Software, GNU Project (Free Software Foundation), by Richard Stallman, published 2007. Read 27 November 2026.

Dated to the first copyright year of the essay (2007); the essay has been revised since and the current text was read.

Connects: Richard Stallman, Free Software Foundation, Why Open Source Misses the Point of Free Software

May 2007 Hostility and adoption

In May 2007 Microsoft's general counsel Brad Smith and licensing chief Horacio Gutierrez told Fortune that free and open source software infringed 235 Microsoft patents.

Revealing the precise figure for the first time, they state that FOSS infringes on no fewer than 235 Microsoft patents.

Secondary source: reporting Microsoft takes on the free world, Fortune, by Roger Parloff, published 28 May 2007. Read 27 November 2026.

Disputed. The figure is often attributed to Steve Ballmer. In the Fortune article it is stated by Brad Smith and Horacio Gutierrez; Ballmer is quoted in the same article saying open source users must "play by the same rules as the rest of the business". Microsoft did not identify the patents, and the claim was never tested in court as far as the sources read here show.

Connects: Brad Smith, Steve Ballmer, Microsoft

29 June 2007 Hostility and adoption

The Free Software Foundation released version 3 of the GPL on 29 June 2007, adding protection against locked-down devices and an explicit patent licence.

Version 3 of the GNU GPL strengthens this guarantee, by ensuring that users can modify the free software on their personal and household devices, and granting patent licenses to every user.

Primary source: press-release FSF releases the GNU General Public License, version 3, Free Software Foundation, published 29 June 2007. Read 27 November 2026.

Connects: Richard Stallman, Free Software Foundation, GNU General Public License
