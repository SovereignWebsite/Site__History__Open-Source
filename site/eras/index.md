# Eras

The story in order. Each era page has a short account and every fact that belongs to it.

- [Shared by default](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/eras/shared-by-default/) (1955 to 1982): Early software travelled as source code among the people who used the machines: IBM's users formed SHARE in 1955 and exchanged their modifications. Unix was born at Bell Labs in 1969 and Berkeley's first distribution went out in 1977. As software became a product, Bill Gates's 1976 letter put the case against sharing.
- [Free software](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/eras/free-software/) (1983 to 1997): Richard Stallman announced GNU in 1983, and the Free Software Foundation and the GPL gave the idea an organisation and a licence. Berkeley freed its code under permissive terms, Linux joined GNU under the copyleft, and Debian and Apache showed that volunteers could run large projects. In 1997 Debian defined free software in its Social Contract and Eric Raymond described the method in "The Cathedral and the Bazaar".
- [The label](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/eras/the-label/) (1998 to 2000): Netscape's decision to publish its browser source prompted a new name, "open source", proposed by Christine Peterson and promoted by Eric Raymond, Bruce Perens and Tim O'Reilly. Stallman's movement kept the name free software. Microsoft's internal view leaked in the Halloween Documents, while Red Hat went public and IBM and Sun committed to open source.
- [Hostility and adoption](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/eras/hostility-and-adoption/) (2001 to 2008): Microsoft's leaders attacked open source and the GPL in public in 2001, advertised against Linux and claimed patent infringement; SCO sued IBM over Linux. In the same years Firefox, Git, Android and GitHub appeared, Sun put Java under the GPL and the GPL reached version 3.
- [Mainstream](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/eras/mainstream/) (2009 to 2017): In these years even the largest opponent joined in: Microsoft contributed to the Linux kernel and, under Satya Nadella, declared that it loved Linux, opened .NET and Visual Studio Code and joined the Linux Foundation. Google open sourced Kubernetes, which then moved to a foundation. Heartbleed showed the cost of depending on underfunded projects.
- [Strain and stewardship](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/eras/strain-and-stewardship/) (2018 to 2026): Two of the largest open source companies were bought, GitHub by Microsoft and Red Hat by IBM. Vendors squeezed by cloud providers left open source licences and some returned. Supply-chain attacks and vulnerabilities brought foundations and regulators in, and the meaning of "open source" for AI is contested.
