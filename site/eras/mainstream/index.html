Mainstream

2009 to 2017.

In these years even the largest opponent joined in: Microsoft contributed to the Linux kernel1 and, under Satya Nadella, declared that it loved Linux23, opened .NET and Visual Studio Code45 and joined the Linux Foundation6. Google open sourced Kubernetes, which then moved to a foundation78. Heartbleed showed the cost of depending on underfunded projects9.

10 September 2009 Mainstream

In September 2009 Microsoft sponsored the new CodePlex Foundation with $1 million, to help software companies and open source communities exchange code.

The Foundation's mission to help the exchange of code and understanding between software companies and open source communities is really interesting to Microsoft. To help the Foundation fund its first year of operations, Microsoft is donating U.S. $1 million

Primary source: own-post The CodePlex Foundation Debuts, Microsoft (Port 25 blog, archived on Microsoft Learn), by Peter Galli, published 10 September 2009. Read 27 November 2026.

From Microsoft's Port 25 blog, quoting Bill Staples of Microsoft. The foundation was later renamed the Outercurve Foundation; no primary source for the renaming or for the 2006 launch of the CodePlex hosting site was fetched.

Connects: Microsoft, CodePlex Foundation

2 August 2010 Mainstream

An early practitioner definition of DevOps (2010) traces it to "agile infrastructure": applying Agile and Lean approaches to operations work.

DevOps emerged from the collision of two major related trends. The first was called “agile infrastructure” or “agile operations”; it sprang from applying Agile and Lean approaches to operations work.

Secondary source: reporting What Is DevOps?, the agile admin, by Ernest Mueller, published 2 August 2010. Read 27 November 2026.

A practitioner's blog, used as a contemporary secondary description; it is one view of the origins.

7 April 2014 Mainstream

In April 2014 the Heartbleed bug in the open source OpenSSL library exposed information protected by the encryption that secures much of the internet.

The Heartbleed Bug is a serious vulnerability in the popular OpenSSL cryptographic software library. This weakness allows stealing the information protected, under normal conditions, by the SSL/TLS encryption used to secure the Internet.

Primary source: report The Heartbleed Bug, Codenomicon (heartbleed.com), published 2014. Read 27 November 2026.

The date is that of the fixed release: the same page says "OpenSSL 1.0.1g released on 7th of April 2014 fixes the bug."

Connects: Heartbleed
