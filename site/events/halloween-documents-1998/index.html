Leak of the Halloween Documents

Publication by Eric Raymond of leaked Microsoft memoranda on open source12.

The facts

11 August 1998 The label

The same memorandum concluded that open source was credible in the long term and could not be fought with fear, uncertainty and doubt.

OSS is long-term credible ... FUD tactics can not be used to combat it.

Primary source: document Halloween Document I (Microsoft memorandum "Open Source Software: A (New?) Development Methodology", annotated), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Vinod Valloppillil (memorandum); Eric S. Raymond (annotations), published 1 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

Quoted from Raymond's list of "key quotes", where the ellipsis is his; the full sentence is further down in the memorandum.

11 August 1998 The label

An internal Microsoft memorandum of August 1998 judged open source software to be a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to the company.

OSS poses a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to Microsoft, particularly in server space.

Primary source: document Halloween Document I (Microsoft memorandum "Open Source Software: A (New?) Development Methodology", annotated), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Vinod Valloppillil (memorandum); Eric S. Raymond (annotations), published 1 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

The memorandum (author Vinod Valloppillil, dated "Aug 11, 1998 -- v1.00" in the text) was leaked to Eric Raymond, who published it with his annotations on 1 November 1998. It is read here in Raymond's annotated copy; Microsoft has not published the original.

Connects: Vinod Valloppillil, Eric S. Raymond, Microsoft, Halloween Document I, Leak of the Halloween Documents

November 1998 The label

According to Raymond, Microsoft admitted that the leaked memorandum was authentic within days of its publication.

Pressed, Microsoft admitted the authenticity of Halloween I later on Monday

Primary source: own-post Halloween Document III: Microsoft's Reaction to the "Halloween Memorandum", Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Eric S. Raymond, published 5 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

Usually paraphrased. This is Raymond's statement, written on 5 November 1998, not Microsoft's own words. The same page reproduces a response attributed to Microsoft Netherlands. No Microsoft press statement from 1998 could be fetched.

Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Microsoft, Leak of the Halloween Documents
