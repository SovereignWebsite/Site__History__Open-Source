1955 Shared by default

In the early mainframe years IBM supplied its software as source code, and SHARE members modified it and exchanged their changes through the SHARE library.

Originally, IBM distributed what software it provided in source form [...] and systems programmers commonly made small local additions or modifications and exchanged them with other users.

Secondary source: encyclopaedia SHARE (computing), Wikipedia. Read 27 November 2026.

Encyclopaedia entry used as a last resort: no primary SHARE or IBM document on the library could be fetched. The date is the founding year of SHARE, not of a particular exchange.

Connects: SHARE, IBM

12 December 2000 The label

In December 2000 IBM's chief executive, Lou Gerstner, said IBM would spend $1 billion on Linux in 2001.

Chief executive Louis Gerstner says Big Blue will spend $1 billion on Linux next year

Secondary source: reporting IBM to spend $1 billion on Linux in 2001, CNET, by CNET staff, published 12 December 2000. Read 27 November 2026.

Reported by CNET on 12 December 2000 from Gerstner's speech at the eBusiness Conference and Expo in New York. No IBM transcript of the speech was found.

Connects: Louis V. Gerstner, IBM

2003 Hostility and adoption

The SCO Group's lawsuit against IBM over Unix code and Linux began in 2003.

The case commenced in 2003, but its roots go even deeper.

Secondary source: reporting Ancient 'Who owns Unix?' case puts a $14.25M price tag on making some claims go away, The Register, published 30 August 2021. Read 27 November 2026.

Secondary reporting from 2021; the 2003 complaint itself was not fetched (Groklaw and the court sites refused the connection).

28 October 2018 Strain and stewardship

On 28 October 2018 IBM announced it would acquire Red Hat for about $34 billion.

IBM will acquire all of the issued and outstanding common shares of Red Hat for $190.00 per share in cash, representing a total enterprise value of approximately $34 billion.

Primary source: press-release IBM To Acquire Red Hat, Completely Changing The Cloud Landscape And Becoming World's #1 Hybrid Cloud Provider, Red Hat and IBM, published 28 October 2018. Read 27 November 2026.

9 July 2019 Strain and stewardship

IBM closed its acquisition of Red Hat on 9 July 2019.

IBM (NYSE:IBM) and Red Hat announced today that they have closed the transaction under which IBM acquired all of the issued and outstanding common shares of Red Hat for $190.00 per share in cash, representing a total equity value of approximately $34 billion.

Primary source: press-release IBM Closes Landmark Acquisition of Red Hat for $34 Billion; Defines Open, Hybrid Cloud Future, Red Hat and IBM, published 9 July 2019. Read 27 November 2026.

3 August 2020 Strain and stewardship

In August 2020 the Linux Foundation formed the Open Source Security Foundation, folding in the Core Infrastructure Initiative; Microsoft, Google, IBM, Red Hat and GitHub were among its founding board members.

It combines efforts from the Core Infrastructure Initiative, GitHub’s Open Source Security Coalition and other open source security work from founding governing board members GitHub, Google, IBM, JPMorgan Chase, Microsoft, NCC Group, OWASP Foundation and Red Hat, among others.

Primary source: press-release Technology and Enterprise Leaders Combine Efforts to Improve Open Source Security, Open Source Security Foundation (The Linux Foundation), published 3 August 2020. Read 27 November 2026.

Connects: The Linux Foundation, Open Source Security Foundation, GitHub, Google, IBM, Microsoft, Red Hat

November 2021 Strain and stewardship

In November 2021 the federal court in Utah dismissed the last of SCO v. IBM after a settlement, some eighteen years after the case began.

All claims and counterclaims in this action, whether alleged or not alleged, pleaded or not pleaded, have been settled, compromised, and resolved in full, and are DISMISSED with prejudice and on the merits.

Secondary source: reporting Last of original SCO v IBM Linux lawsuit settled, ZDNET, by Steven Vaughan-Nichols, published 8 November 2021. Read 27 November 2026.

The court's order is quoted as reproduced by ZDNET (8 November 2021). The same report quotes the bankruptcy court filing giving the payment as "$14,250,000". The court documents themselves were not fetched.

Connects: The SCO Group, IBM, SCO v. IBM
