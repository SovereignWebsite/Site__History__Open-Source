# Organisations

Every organisation here is linked from at least one sourced fact.

- [.NET Foundation](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/dotnet-foundation/) (1 fact, 2014): Foundation through which Microsoft said it would work with the community on open source .NET.
- [Apache Group and Apache Software Foundation](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/apache/) (3 facts, 1995 to 2021): The group behind the Apache web server (1995), incorporated as a foundation in 1999.
- [Bell Labs](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/bell-labs/) (1 fact, 1969): Research laboratory where Unix was created in 1969.
- [Canonical](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/canonical/) (1 fact, 2016): Maker of Ubuntu and Microsoft's partner for the Windows Subsystem for Linux.
- [Cloud Native Computing Foundation](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/cncf/) (2 facts, 2015 to 2016): Foundation announced in 2015 that took on Kubernetes in 2016.
- [CodePlex Foundation](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/codeplex-foundation/) (1 fact, 2009): Open source foundation sponsored by Microsoft in 2009.
- [Debian Project](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/debian/) (3 facts, 1993 to 1998): Volunteer-run distribution founded in 1993, whose guidelines became the basis of the Open Source Definition.
- [Elastic](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/elastic/) (4 facts, 2021 to 2024): Search company that left the Apache licence in 2021 and added the AGPL in 2024.
- [European Union](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/european-union/) (4 facts, 2024): Legislator of the Cyber Resilience Act, in force since December 2024.
- [Foresight Institute](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/foresight-institute/) (1 fact, 1998): Think tank where Christine Peterson worked when she proposed "open source".
- [Free Software Foundation](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/fsf/) (9 facts, 1985 to 2024): Foundation set up in October 1985 to support GNU; publisher of the GPL.
- [GitHub](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/github/) (11 facts, 2008 to 2026): Code hosting service launched in 2008, owned by Microsoft since 2018.
- [GNU Project](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/gnu-project/) (5 facts, 1983 to 1996): Project announced by Stallman in 1983 to build a complete free Unix-compatible system.
- [Google](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/google/) (5 facts, 2005 to 2020): Company behind Summer of Code, Android and Kubernetes.
- [HashiCorp](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/hashicorp/) (2 facts, 2023): Infrastructure software company that moved Terraform to the Business Source License in 2023.
- [IBM](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/ibm/) (7 facts, 1955 to 2021): Computer company that shipped early software as source, committed $1 billion to Linux in 2000 and bought Red Hat in 2019.
- [Jenkins](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/jenkins/) (3 facts, 2011 to 2024): Open source continuous integration server, renamed from Hudson by a community vote in 2011.
- [Kubernetes](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/kubernetes/) (3 facts, 2014 to 2016): Container orchestration project open sourced by Google in 2014.
- [Microsoft](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/microsoft/) (34 facts, 1998 to 2025): Software company that opposed open source around 2001 and later joined the Linux Foundation and bought GitHub.
- [Microsoft Open Technologies, Inc.](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/ms-open-tech/) (1 fact, 2012): Microsoft subsidiary for open standards and open source, announced in 2012.
- [MIT Artificial Intelligence Lab](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/mit-ai-lab/) (1 fact, 1971): Laboratory whose software-sharing community Stallman joined in 1971.
- [MongoDB, Inc.](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/mongodb/) (5 facts, 2018 to 2021): Database company that created the Server Side Public License in 2018.
- [Mozilla](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/mozilla/) (2 facts, 1998 to 2004): Project created in 1998 from the Netscape source, later a foundation and the maker of Firefox.
- [Netscape Communications](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/netscape/) (4 facts, 1998): Browser company whose January 1998 decision to publish its source prompted the "open source" label.
- [O'Reilly & Associates](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/oreilly/) (1 fact, 1998): Publisher that hosted the April 1998 summit.
- [Open Handset Alliance](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/open-handset-alliance/) (2 facts, 2007 to 2008): Alliance of companies that announced Android in 2007.
- [Open Invention Network](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/oin/) (2 facts, 2018): Patent non-aggression community for Linux, which Microsoft joined in 2018.
- [Open Source Initiative](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/osi/) (19 facts, 1998 to 2025): Organisation founded in February 1998 by Raymond and Perens; keeper of the Open Source Definition and, since 2024, the Open Source AI Definition.
- [Open Source Security Foundation](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/openssf/) (2 facts, 2014 to 2020): Linux Foundation body formed in 2020 for the security of open source software.
- [OpenTofu](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/opentofu/) (2 facts, 2023): Open source alternative to Terraform under the Linux Foundation.
- [Red Hat](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/red-hat/) (4 facts, 1999 to 2020): Linux company that went public in 1999 and was acquired by IBM in 2019.
- [Redis](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/redis/) (4 facts, 2024 to 2025): Database company that left the BSD licence in 2024 and added the AGPL in 2025.
- [SHARE](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/share/) (2 facts, 1955): User group of IBM computer installations, begun in 1955.
- [Software Freedom Conservancy](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/sfc/) (1 fact, 2024): Non-profit that criticised the Open Source AI Definition in 2024.
- [Sun Microsystems](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/sun/) (2 facts, 2000 to 2006): Company that opened StarOffice in 2000 and committed Java to the GPL in 2006.
- [The Linux Foundation](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/linux-foundation/) (6 facts, 2014 to 2024): Non-profit that hosts Linux and many other projects, including the CNCF, OpenSSF, OpenTofu and Valkey.
- [The SCO Group](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/sco/) (2 facts, 2003 to 2021): Company that sued IBM over Unix and Linux in 2003 in a case dismissed in 2021.
- [Thoughtworks](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/thoughtworks/) (1 fact, 2024): Consultancy whose staff built CruiseControl, by Martin Fowler's account.
- [University of California, Berkeley (Computer Systems Research Group)](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/uc-berkeley-csrg/) (4 facts, 1977 to 1994): Home of the Berkeley Software Distribution and its permissive licence.
- [Valkey](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/organisations/valkey/) (1 fact, 2024): Linux Foundation project continuing Redis 7.2.4 under the BSD licence.
