# People

Public, professional facts only, each with its source.

- [Andres Freund](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/andres-freund/) (1 fact, 2024): Developer who found and reported the xz backdoor in March 2024.
- [Bill Gates](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/bill-gates/) (1 fact, 1976): Co-founder of Microsoft and author of the 1976 "Open Letter to Hobbyists".
- [Bill Joy](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/bill-joy/) (1 fact, 1977): Berkeley graduate student who put together the first Berkeley Software Distribution in 1977.
- [Brad Smith](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/brad-smith/) (2 facts, 2007 to 2020): Microsoft general counsel in 2007 and later its president, who said in 2020 that the company had been on the wrong side of history on open source.
- [Brian Behlendorf](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/brian-behlendorf/) (1 fact, 1995): One of the eight founders of the Apache Group in 1995.
- [Bruce Perens](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/bruce-perens/) (6 facts, 1997 to 1999): Debian project leader who drafted the Debian Free Software Guidelines and edited them into the Open Source Definition; co-founder of the OSI.
- [Christine Peterson](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/christine-peterson/) (4 facts, 1998): Executive director of the Foresight Institute in 1998, who proposed the term "open source software".
- [Craig Mundie](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/craig-mundie/) (2 facts, 2001): Microsoft senior vice president who set out the company's "Shared Source" position in May 2001.
- [Dennis Ritchie](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/dennis-ritchie/) (1 fact, 1969): Bell Labs computer scientist, co-creator of Unix and author of its early history.
- [Eric S. Raymond](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/eric-raymond/) (13 facts, 1997 to 2000): Author of "The Cathedral and the Bazaar", co-founder of the Open Source Initiative and publisher of the Halloween Documents.
- [Erich Gamma](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/erich-gamma/) (1 fact, 1997): Co-author, with Kent Beck, of the JUnit testing framework.
- [Ian Murdock](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/ian-murdock/) (1 fact, 1993): Founder of the Debian Project in 1993.
- [Jim Allchin](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/jim-allchin/) (2 facts, 2001): Head of the Windows operating system at Microsoft in 2001, who called open source an intellectual-property destroyer.
- [Kent Beck](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/kent-beck/) (3 facts, 1997 to 2001): Co-author of the JUnit testing framework and an author of the Agile Manifesto.
- [Kohsuke Kawaguchi](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/kohsuke-kawaguchi/) (2 facts, 2011): Creator of the Hudson continuous integration server, which its community renamed Jenkins in 2011.
- [Linus Torvalds](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/linus-torvalds/) (8 facts, 1991 to 2005): Creator of the Linux kernel (1991) and of Git (2005).
- [Louis V. Gerstner](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/lou-gerstner/) (1 fact, 2000): Chief executive of IBM who announced in December 2000 that the company would spend $1 billion on Linux.
- [Marshall Kirk McKusick](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/kirk-mckusick/) (1 fact, 1977): Member of Berkeley's Computer Systems Research Group and author of the history of Berkeley Unix used here.
- [Martin Fowler](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/martin-fowler/) (8 facts, 1997 to 2024): Author of "The New Methodology" and "Continuous Integration" and an author of the Agile Manifesto.
- [Richard Stallman](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/richard-stallman/) (9 facts, 1971 to 2007): Programmer who announced the GNU Project in 1983 and founded the free software movement.
- [Satya Nadella](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/satya-nadella/) (6 facts, 2014 to 2018): Chief executive of Microsoft from February 2014, who presented the "Microsoft loves Linux" slide that October.
- [Scott Guthrie](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/scott-guthrie/) (1 fact, 2016): Microsoft executive vice president who announced SQL Server on Linux in 2016.
- [Shay Banon](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/shay-banon/) (3 facts, 2021 to 2024): Elastic executive who announced its licence change in 2021 and its return to an open source licence in 2024.
- [Steve Ballmer](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/steve-ballmer/) (6 facts, 2001 to 2016): Chief executive of Microsoft in 2001, reported in 2001 as calling Linux a cancer in an intellectual property sense.
- [Tim O'Reilly](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/tim-oreilly/) (2 facts, 1998): Publisher who organised the April 1998 summit at which leading developers agreed on the term "open source".
- [Todd Anderson](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/todd-anderson/) (1 fact, 1998): Linux programmer who, in Peterson's account, introduced her term at the February 1998 meeting.
- [Vinod Valloppillil](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/vinod-valloppillil/) (2 facts, 1998): Microsoft employee named as author of the 1998 memorandum on open source software known as Halloween Document I.
- [Ward Cunningham](https://open-source.history.sovereignweb.site/people/ward-cunningham/) (5 facts, 1995 to 2026): Programmer who created the first wiki in 1995 and is an author of the Agile Manifesto.
