## The facts

### 1997: Kent Beck and Erich Gamma wrote the first version of the JUnit testing framework together on a flight to the OOPSLA conference in 1997.

> JUnit was born on a flight from Zurich to the 1997 OOPSLA in Atlanta. Kent was flying with Erich Gamma, and what else were two geeks to do on a long flight but program?

- Source (secondary, own-post): [Xunit](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/Xunit.html), martinfowler.com, published 17 January 2006. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Martin Fowler's account, written in 2006; he says he was one of the first users.
- Connects: Kent Beck, Erich Gamma, Martin Fowler, JUnit
- Fact id: `junit-1997`

### July 2000: Fowler picked out the same point Raymond had made in "Linus's Law": in open source, debugging can be done by many people in parallel.

> A particular feature of open source development is that debugging is highly parallelizable.

- Source (primary, own-post): [The New Methodology (Original)](https://martinfowler.com/articles/newMethodologyOriginal.html), martinfowler.com, published 21 July 2000. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Martin Fowler, Eric S. Raymond, The New Methodology, The Cathedral and the Bazaar
- Fact id: `fowler-open-source-debugging-2000`

### July 2000: In the original July 2000 version of "The New Methodology", Martin Fowler listed open source alongside the new lightweight methods, saying much of its way of working applied to closed source projects too.

> However there is a definite way of doing things in the open source community, and much of their approach is as applicable to closed source projects as it is to open source.

- Source (primary, own-post): [The New Methodology (Original)](https://martinfowler.com/articles/newMethodologyOriginal.html), martinfowler.com, published 21 July 2000. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Fowler republished the original text himself; the current version of the article no longer has the section.
- Connects: Martin Fowler, The New Methodology
- Fact id: `fowler-open-source-new-methodology-2000`

### 10 September 2000: The original "Continuous Integration" article by Martin Fowler and Matthew Foemmel (September 2000) records their team moving its builds to Ant, an open source tool.

> Recently, however, we have switched over to the open-source build tool Ant.

- Source (primary, own-post): [Continuous Integration (original version)](https://martinfowler.com/articles/originalContinuousIntegration.html), martinfowler.com, published 10 September 2000. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Martin Fowler
- Fact id: `continuous-integration-ant-2000`

### February 2001: The authors of the Agile Manifesto include Kent Beck (JUnit), Ward Cunningham (the first wiki) and Martin Fowler (who wrote on open source as a method).

> Kent Beck [...] Ward Cunningham Martin Fowler

- Source (primary, document): [Manifesto for Agile Software Development](https://agilemanifesto.org/), agilemanifesto.org, published February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Kent Beck, Ward Cunningham, Martin Fowler, Manifesto for Agile Software Development
- Fact id: `agile-manifesto-authors-2001`

### February 2001: The Manifesto for Agile Software Development carries a notice that it may be freely copied in any form, but only in its entirety.

> © 2001, the above authors this declaration may be freely copied in any form, but only in its entirety through this notice.

- Source (primary, document): [Manifesto for Agile Software Development](https://agilemanifesto.org/), agilemanifesto.org, published February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: This permits verbatim copying only, so it is not an open source or free culture licence; the parallel is in the wish to be copied.
- Connects: Kent Beck, Ward Cunningham, Martin Fowler, Manifesto for Agile Software Development
- Fact id: `agile-manifesto-freely-copied-2001`

### 18 January 2024: In his 2024 revision of "Continuous Integration", Fowler names the open source Jenkins and GitHub Actions among the usual continuous integration services.

> (Examples of CI Services are tools like Jenkins, GitHub Actions, Circle CI etc.)

- Source (primary, own-post): [Continuous Integration](https://martinfowler.com/articles/continuousIntegration.html), martinfowler.com, published 18 January 2024. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Fowler does not say here that Jenkins is open source; the Jenkins project describes itself as "the leading open source automation server" on its home page.
- Connects: Martin Fowler, Jenkins, GitHub
- Fact id: `ci-services-open-source-2024`

### 18 January 2024: Fowler credits his colleagues at Thoughtworks with CruiseControl, which he calls the first continuous integration service and which was open source.

> my colleagues at Thoughtworks, have contributed a lot of open-source tooling for Continuous Integration, in particular Cruise Control - the first CI Service.

- Source (primary, own-post): [Continuous Integration](https://martinfowler.com/articles/continuousIntegration.html), martinfowler.com, published 18 January 2024. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: From the 2024 revision of Fowler's article, and dated to that revision because the article gives no year for CruiseControl. The project's SourceForge page shows it registered on 2001-03-23 (see cruisecontrol-bsd-licence).
- Connects: Martin Fowler, Thoughtworks
- Fact id: `cruisecontrol-open-source`
