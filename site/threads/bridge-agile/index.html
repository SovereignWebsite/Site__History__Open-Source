Where open source meets Agile

The two histories share people, tools and one habit: put working software in front of others early and often. Each step here is a point where they touch.

  1. 25 March 1995 Free software

    A future Agile Manifesto author starts the first wiki.

    Ward Cunningham started the first wiki, the WikiWikiWeb, on 25 March 1995 as a supplement to the Portland Pattern Repository.

    This is the first ever wiki site, founded as an automated supplement to the PortlandPatternRepository.

    Primary source: archive WikiHistory (page of the original wiki, WikiWikiWeb), Cunningham & Cunningham, Inc. (c2.com), by Ward Cunningham and wiki contributors. Read 27 November 2026.

    From the WikiHistory page of the original wiki, read from the site's page data (JSON). The page opens "This began on March 25, 1995." and continues in the first person ("I created the site"); it is a wiki page and so could have been edited by others.

    Connects: Ward Cunningham, WikiWikiWeb

  2. 1997 Free software

    Two programmers write JUnit, the tool behind test-first development.

    Kent Beck and Erich Gamma wrote the first version of the JUnit testing framework together on a flight to the OOPSLA conference in 1997.

    JUnit was born on a flight from Zurich to the 1997 OOPSLA in Atlanta. Kent was flying with Erich Gamma, and what else were two geeks to do on a long flight but program?

    Secondary source: own-post Xunit, martinfowler.com, by Martin Fowler, published 17 January 2006. Read 27 November 2026.

    Martin Fowler's account, written in 2006; he says he was one of the first users.

    Connects: Kent Beck, Erich Gamma, Martin Fowler, JUnit

    Link to this fact

  3. May 1997 Free software

    Raymond states the bazaar's rule: release early, release often.

    Lesson 7 of "The Cathedral and the Bazaar" is to release early, release often and listen to your customers, which Raymond drew from how Torvalds ran Linux.

    7. Release early. Release often. And listen to your customers.

    Primary source: document The Cathedral and the Bazaar: Release Early, Release Often, Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Eric S. Raymond, published May 1997. Read 27 November 2026.

    Text read is version 3.0 of the essay (2000); the lesson is commonly cited from the 1997 original.

    Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Linus Torvalds, The Cathedral and the Bazaar

    Link to this fact

  4. July 2000 The label

    Fowler lists open source among the new methods.

    In the original July 2000 version of "The New Methodology", Martin Fowler listed open source alongside the new lightweight methods, saying much of its way of working applied to closed source projects too.

    However there is a definite way of doing things in the open source community, and much of their approach is as applicable to closed source projects as it is to open source.

    Primary source: own-post The New Methodology (Original), martinfowler.com, by Martin Fowler, published 21 July 2000. Read 27 November 2026.

    Fowler republished the original text himself; the current version of the article no longer has the section.

    Connects: Martin Fowler, The New Methodology

    Link to this fact

  5. July 2000 The label

    He notes that its debugging runs in parallel.

    Fowler picked out the same point Raymond had made in "Linus's Law": in open source, debugging can be done by many people in parallel.

    A particular feature of open source development is that debugging is highly parallelizable.

    Primary source: own-post The New Methodology (Original), martinfowler.com, by Martin Fowler, published 21 July 2000. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Martin Fowler, Eric S. Raymond, The New Methodology, The Cathedral and the Bazaar

    Link to this fact

  6. 10 September 2000 The label

    The first continuous integration article builds on an open source tool.

    The original "Continuous Integration" article by Martin Fowler and Matthew Foemmel (September 2000) records their team moving its builds to Ant, an open source tool.

    Recently, however, we have switched over to the open-source build tool Ant.

    Primary source: own-post Continuous Integration (original version), martinfowler.com, by Martin Fowler and Matthew Foemmel, published 10 September 2000. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Martin Fowler

    Link to this fact

  7. February 2001 Hostility and adoption

    Beck, Cunningham and Fowler sign the Agile Manifesto.

    The authors of the Agile Manifesto include Kent Beck (JUnit), Ward Cunningham (the first wiki) and Martin Fowler (who wrote on open source as a method).

    Kent Beck [...] Ward Cunningham Martin Fowler

    Primary source: document Manifesto for Agile Software Development, agilemanifesto.org, by The seventeen authors, published February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Kent Beck, Ward Cunningham, Martin Fowler, Manifesto for Agile Software Development

    Link to this fact

  8. February 2001 Hostility and adoption

    The Manifesto asks to be copied freely.

    The Manifesto for Agile Software Development carries a notice that it may be freely copied in any form, but only in its entirety.

    © 2001, the above authors this declaration may be freely copied in any form, but only in its entirety through this notice.

    Primary source: document Manifesto for Agile Software Development, agilemanifesto.org, by The seventeen authors, published February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    This permits verbatim copying only, so it is not an open source or free culture licence; the parallel is in the wish to be copied.

    Connects: Kent Beck, Ward Cunningham, Martin Fowler, Manifesto for Agile Software Development

    Link to this fact

  9. 23 March 2001 Hostility and adoption

    The first continuous integration server is an open source project.

    The CruiseControl continuous integration tool was registered as a project on SourceForge on 23 March 2001, under a BSD licence.

    BSD License [...] Registered [...] 2001-03-23

    Primary source: archive CruiseControl project summary, SourceForge. Read 27 November 2026.

    From the project's SourceForge summary page, which lists the licence and the registration date as separate fields. The project home page says: "CruiseControl is open source software" and "distributed under a BSD-style license".

    Connects: BSD licence

    Link to this fact

  10. 2005 Hostility and adoption

    The Linux kernel's needs produce Git.

    Git was created in 2005 by Linus Torvalds and the Linux kernel community after they lost free use of the proprietary BitKeeper tool.

    In 2005, the relationship between the community that developed the Linux kernel and the commercial company that developed BitKeeper broke down, and the tool’s free-of-charge status was revoked. This prompted the Linux development community (and in particular Linus Torvalds, the creator of Linux) to develop their own tool

    Secondary source: book Pro Git, 1.2 Getting Started: A Short History of Git, git-scm.com (Apress), by Scott Chacon and Ben Straub. Read 27 November 2026.

    From the Pro Git book hosted on the Git project's site. Torvalds's own 2005 messages on lore.kernel.org were behind a bot challenge and could not be read.

    Connects: Linus Torvalds, Git

  11. 23 February 2008 Hostility and adoption

    GitHub adds the pull request.

    GitHub added pull requests in February 2008, while the site was still in beta.

    Last night I pushed out a feature Tom and I have been talking about since day one: pull requests.

    Primary source: own-post Oh yeah, there's pull requests now, The GitHub Blog, by Chris Wanstrath, published 23 February 2008. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: GitHub, Git

    Link to this fact

  12. 2009 Mainstream

    DevOps gets its conference.

    The first devopsdays conference was held in Ghent, Belgium, in 2009.

    The first devopsdays was held in Ghent, Belgium in 2009.

    Primary source: document About devopsdays, devopsdays. Read 27 November 2026.

    It is usually said that this conference gave the DevOps movement its name; that is not stated on this page and is not claimed here.

    Connects: First devopsdays

    Link to this fact

  13. 2 August 2010 Mainstream

    DevOps is described as Agile applied to operations.

    An early practitioner definition of DevOps (2010) traces it to "agile infrastructure": applying Agile and Lean approaches to operations work.

    DevOps emerged from the collision of two major related trends. The first was called “agile infrastructure” or “agile operations”; it sprang from applying Agile and Lean approaches to operations work.

    Secondary source: reporting What Is DevOps?, the agile admin, by Ernest Mueller, published 2 August 2010. Read 27 November 2026.

    A practitioner's blog, used as a contemporary secondary description; it is one view of the origins.

    Link to this fact

  14. 29 January 2011 Mainstream

    The Hudson community votes to become Jenkins.

    In January 2011 the community behind the Hudson continuous integration server voted 214 to 14 to rename the project Jenkins.

    The final result of all eligible votes is as follows: 214 votes to rename, and 14 for the status quo.

    Primary source: own-post Jenkins!, Jenkins project blog, by Andrew Bayer, published 29 January 2011. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Kohsuke Kawaguchi, Jenkins

    Link to this fact

  15. 18 January 2024 Strain and stewardship

    Jenkins and GitHub Actions are named as usual continuous integration services.

    In his 2024 revision of "Continuous Integration", Fowler names the open source Jenkins and GitHub Actions among the usual continuous integration services.

    (Examples of CI Services are tools like Jenkins, GitHub Actions, Circle CI etc.)

    Primary source: own-post Continuous Integration, martinfowler.com, by Martin Fowler, published 18 January 2024. Read 27 November 2026.

    Fowler does not say here that Jenkins is open source; the Jenkins project describes itself as "the leading open source automation server" on its home page.

    Connects: Martin Fowler, Jenkins, GitHub
