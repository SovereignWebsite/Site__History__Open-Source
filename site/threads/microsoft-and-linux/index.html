Microsoft and Linux: from "a cancer" to "Microsoft loves Linux"

One company's change of position, step by step, each step from a document: the leaked memo, the speeches and interviews of 2001, the first kernel contribution, the 2014 slide, and what followed.

  1. 11 August 1998 The label

    1998: an internal memo sees a revenue and platform threat.

    An internal Microsoft memorandum of August 1998 judged open source software to be a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to the company.

    OSS poses a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to Microsoft, particularly in server space.

    Primary source: document Halloween Document I (Microsoft memorandum "Open Source Software: A (New?) Development Methodology", annotated), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Vinod Valloppillil (memorandum); Eric S. Raymond (annotations), published 1 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    The memorandum (author Vinod Valloppillil, dated "Aug 11, 1998 -- v1.00" in the text) was leaked to Eric Raymond, who published it with his annotations on 1 November 1998. It is read here in Raymond's annotated copy; Microsoft has not published the original.

    Connects: Vinod Valloppillil, Eric S. Raymond, Microsoft, Halloween Document I, Leak of the Halloween Documents

    Link to this fact

  2. February 2001 Hostility and adoption

    February 2001: "an intellectual-property destroyer".

    In February 2001 Jim Allchin, head of Windows at Microsoft, called open source an intellectual-property destroyer.

    ”Open source is an intellectual-property destroyer,” Allchin said. ”I can’t imagine something that could be worse than this for the software business and the intellectual-property business.”

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft exec calls open source a threat to innovation, CNET (Bloomberg News copy), by Bloomberg News, published 15 February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    A Bloomberg News report as carried by CNET on 15 February 2001 ("said this week"). The news report is the record of the words; no transcript is known.

    Connects: Jim Allchin, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  3. 3 May 2001 Hostility and adoption

    May 2001: the GPL is called viral.

    On 3 May 2001 Craig Mundie of Microsoft told an audience at New York University's Stern School of Business that the "viral" GPL threatened the intellectual property of any organisation using it.

    This viral aspect of the GPL poses a threat to the intellectual property of any organization making use of it.

    Primary source: speech Speech Transcript: Craig Mundie, The New York University Stern School of Business (Prepared Text of Remarks: The Commercial Software Model), Microsoft, by Craig Mundie, published 3 May 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    From the prepared text of the remarks as published by Microsoft; it is not a verbatim record of the delivery.

    Connects: Craig Mundie, Microsoft, GNU General Public License, Craig Mundie's speech at NYU Stern

    Link to this fact

  4. 3 May 2001 Hostility and adoption

    Microsoft offers "Shared Source" as its alternative.

    In the same speech Mundie set out Microsoft's alternative, "Shared Source": showing source code to customers and partners while keeping the intellectual property.

    Shared Source is a balanced approach that allows us to share source code with customers and partners while maintaining the intellectual property needed to support a strong software business.

    Primary source: speech Speech Transcript: Craig Mundie, The New York University Stern School of Business (Prepared Text of Remarks: The Commercial Software Model), Microsoft, by Craig Mundie, published 3 May 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Craig Mundie, Microsoft, Craig Mundie's speech at NYU Stern

    Link to this fact

  5. 1 June 2001 Hostility and adoption

    June 2001: "a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense".

    Steve Ballmer, Microsoft's chief executive, told the Chicago Sun-Times on 1 June 2001 that Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches.

    "Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches,"

    Secondary source: reporting Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer', The Register, by Thomas C. Greene, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    The words were said in an interview with the Chicago Sun-Times published on 1 June 2001. That article could not be read: the newspaper's old address returns 404 and the Internet Archive was unreachable from this machine. The quote is taken from The Register's report of 2 June 2001, which quotes the interview. No transcript is known, so the newspaper is the record of the words and The Register is a second-hand record.

    Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview

    Link to this fact

  6. 2 June 2001 Hostility and adoption

    The remark is remembered in a shorter form than it was reported.

    The remark is usually repeated in the short form "Linux is a cancer", which is the headline The Register gave its report, and which drops the qualifier about intellectual property.

    Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer'

    Secondary source: reporting Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer', The Register, by Thomas C. Greene, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    Usually paraphrased. The short form is a headline, not the sentence Ballmer is reported to have said. The fuller sentence, as The Register quotes it, is "Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches".

    Connects: Steve Ballmer, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview

    Link to this fact

  7. 5 January 2004 Hostility and adoption

    2004: an advertising campaign against Linux.

    In January 2004 Microsoft launched "Get the Facts", an advertising campaign arguing the advantages of Windows over Linux.

    Microsoft Corp. on Monday launched a new advertising campaign, referred to as “Get the Facts,” which is designed to give customers information about the advantages of using its Windows operating system versus Linux, its open-source competitor.

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft Takes on Linux in Ad Campaign, eWeek, by Peter Galli, published 5 January 2004. Read 27 November 2026.

    Reported by eWeek on 5 January 2004; no Microsoft press release for the campaign was found.

    Connects: Microsoft, "Get the Facts" campaign

    Link to this fact

  8. May 2007 Hostility and adoption

    2007: the 235 patents claim.

    In May 2007 Microsoft's general counsel Brad Smith and licensing chief Horacio Gutierrez told Fortune that free and open source software infringed 235 Microsoft patents.

    Revealing the precise figure for the first time, they state that FOSS infringes on no fewer than 235 Microsoft patents.

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft takes on the free world, Fortune, by Roger Parloff, published 28 May 2007. Read 27 November 2026.

    Disputed. The figure is often attributed to Steve Ballmer. In the Fortune article it is stated by Brad Smith and Horacio Gutierrez; Ballmer is quoted in the same article saying open source users must "play by the same rules as the rest of the business". Microsoft did not identify the patents, and the claim was never tested in court as far as the sources read here show.

    Connects: Brad Smith, Steve Ballmer, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  9. 20 July 2009 Mainstream

    July 2009: 20,000 lines of driver code for the Linux kernel, under the GPL.

    On 20 July 2009 Microsoft released 20,000 lines of Hyper-V driver code for the Linux kernel under version 2 of the GPL.

    In what many may see as a surprising move, Microsoft today released 20,000 lines of device driver code to the Linux community under the popular General Public Licence v2.

    Primary source: own-post Microsoft Releases Device Driver Code to the Linux Community, Microsoft (Port 25 blog, archived on Microsoft Learn), by Peter Galli, published 20 July 2009. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Microsoft, GNU General Public License

    Link to this fact

  10. 20 July 2009 Mainstream

    Microsoft calls it its first contribution to the kernel.

    Microsoft's own virtualisation blog said this was the first time the company had contributed code to the Linux kernel.

    This is the first time Microsoft is contributing code to the Linux kernel

    Primary source: own-post Linux ICs for Hyper-V and GPLv2, Microsoft (Virtualization blog, first published on TechNet), published 20 July 2009. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  11. 10 September 2009 Mainstream

    September 2009: Microsoft funds an open source foundation.

    In September 2009 Microsoft sponsored the new CodePlex Foundation with $1 million, to help software companies and open source communities exchange code.

    The Foundation's mission to help the exchange of code and understanding between software companies and open source communities is really interesting to Microsoft. To help the Foundation fund its first year of operations, Microsoft is donating U.S. $1 million

    Primary source: own-post The CodePlex Foundation Debuts, Microsoft (Port 25 blog, archived on Microsoft Learn), by Peter Galli, published 10 September 2009. Read 27 November 2026.

    From Microsoft's Port 25 blog, quoting Bill Staples of Microsoft. The foundation was later renamed the Outercurve Foundation; no primary source for the renaming or for the 2006 launch of the CodePlex hosting site was fetched.

    Connects: Microsoft, CodePlex Foundation

    Link to this fact

    2012: a subsidiary for open source and open standards.

    On 12 April 2012 Microsoft announced a subsidiary, Microsoft Open Technologies, Inc., for its work on interoperability, open standards and open source.

    Microsoft has announced a new wholly owned subsidiary known as Microsoft Open Technologies, Inc., to advance the company’s investment in openness – including interoperability, open standards and open source.

    Primary source: own-post Announcing one more way Microsoft will engage with the open source and standards communities, Microsoft (Interoperability blog, archived on Microsoft Learn), by Jean Paoli, published 12 April 2012. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Microsoft, Microsoft Open Technologies, Inc.

    February 2014: Satya Nadella becomes chief executive.

    Microsoft's board appointed Satya Nadella chief executive on 4 February 2014.

    Microsoft Corp. today announced that its Board of Directors has appointed Satya Nadella as Chief Executive Officer and member of the Board of Directors effective immediately.

    Primary source: press-release Microsoft Board names Satya Nadella as CEO, Microsoft, published 4 February 2014. Read 27 November 2026.

    The Register's report of October 2014, also read, calls him "Steve Ballmer's successor".

    Connects: Satya Nadella, Microsoft

  14. 20 October 2014 Mainstream

    20 October 2014: "Microsoft loves Linux".

    At a cloud event for the press in San Francisco on 20 October 2014, Satya Nadella delivered the message "Microsoft loves Linux".

    "Microsoft loves Linux" is generally not something one expects to hear, but that was one of the messages that Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella delivered at a San Francisco media event touting Redmond's cloud offerings on Monday.

    Secondary source: reporting Redmond top man Satya Nadella: 'Microsoft LOVES Linux', The Register, by Neil McAllister, published 21 October 2014. Read 27 November 2026.

    The best records found are press reports by reporters at the event (The Register, PCWorld) and Microsoft's own later acknowledgement of the slide (May 2015). No Microsoft transcript or press release of 20 October 2014 containing the phrase was found.

    Connects: Satya Nadella, Microsoft, Microsoft cloud event, San Francisco

    Link to this fact

  15. 20 October 2014 Mainstream

    The words were a slide with a heart on it.

    PCWorld reported that Nadella stood in front of an image reading "Microsoft [heart] Linux" during the webcast.

    This was the message from Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, standing in front of an image that read “Microsoft [heart symbol] Linux,” during a Monday webcast

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft (hearts) Linux, for Azure's sake, PCWorld (IDG News Service), by Joab Jackson, published 20 October 2014. Read 27 November 2026.

    The slide used a heart symbol, so "Microsoft loves Linux" is the spoken reading of it. "[heart symbol]" is PCWorld's own wording.

    Connects: Satya Nadella, Microsoft, Microsoft cloud event, San Francisco

    Link to this fact

  16. 20 October 2014 Mainstream

    The stated reason: about a fifth of Azure already ran Linux.

    At the same event Nadella said about a fifth of Azure was running Linux.

    About 20 percent of the workloads run on Azure are based on Linux, Nadella admitted.

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft (hearts) Linux, for Azure's sake, PCWorld (IDG News Service), by Joab Jackson, published 20 October 2014. Read 27 November 2026.

    Usually paraphrased. A reporter's summary, not a direct quotation. The Register's report says "20 per cent of Microsoft's Azure cloud is already Linux"; the two reports differ on whether the figure is workloads or the cloud as a whole.

    Connects: Satya Nadella, Microsoft, Microsoft cloud event, San Francisco

  17. 12 November 2014 Mainstream

    November 2014: .NET is opened and brought to Linux.

    On 12 November 2014 Microsoft announced it was open sourcing the server-side .NET stack and bringing .NET to Linux and Mac OS, working through the .NET Foundation.

    Microsoft Corp. reinforced its commitment to cross-platform developer experiences by open sourcing the full server-side .NET stack and expanding .NET to run on the Linux and Mac OS platforms.

    Primary source: press-release Microsoft takes .NET open source and cross-platform, adds new development capabilities with Visual Studio 2015, .NET 2015 and Visual Studio Online, Microsoft, published 12 November 2014. Read 27 November 2026.

    The same release says Microsoft "will work through the .NET Foundation".

    Connects: Microsoft, .NET Foundation

    Microsoft's Windows Server team confirmed the slide in its own blog post in May 2015.

    In a press and analyst briefing a few months back, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella put up a slide proclaiming “Microsoft ♥ Linux”.

    Primary source: own-post Microsoft Loves Linux, Microsoft (Windows Server blog), by Microsoft Windows Server Team, published 6 May 2015. Read 27 November 2026.

    Microsoft released Visual Studio Code beta as an open source project, available on GitHub.

  21. 9 March 2016 Mainstream

    The same week Ballmer says the Linux threat is in the rearview mirror.

    In March 2016 Steve Ballmer said his earlier stance on Linux had been right for its time but that the threat from Linux was now "in the rearview mirror".

    Ballmer said the position was right for the time, but the threat from Linux was now "in the rearview mirror."

    Secondary source: reporting Former Microsoft CEO Ballmer does about-face on Linux technology, Reuters (as carried by The Star, Malaysia), by Reuters, published 11 March 2016. Read 27 November 2026.

    Usually paraphrased. A Reuters report, read as carried by The Star (Malaysia) because reuters.com returned 401; only the first two paragraphs were readable there. Only "in the rearview mirror" is a direct quotation; "right for the time" is the reporter's paraphrase. The remarks were made at a dinner hosted by Fortune on 9 March 2016; no transcript is known.

    Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft

  22. 30 March 2016 Mainstream

    March 2016: Linux tools run inside Windows.

    On 30 March 2016 Microsoft announced the Windows Subsystem for Linux, which runs Ubuntu's Bash shell and Linux command-line tools on Windows.

    we built new infrastructure within Windows – the Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL) – upon which we run a genuine Ubuntu user-mode image provided by our great partners over at Canonical, creators of Ubuntu Linux.

    Primary source: own-post Run Bash on Ubuntu on Windows, Windows Developer Blog (Microsoft), by Mike Harsh, published 30 March 2016. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Microsoft, Canonical

    Primary source: press-release Microsoft Fortifies Commitment to Open Source, Becomes Linux Foundation Platinum Member, The Linux Foundation, published 16 November 2016. Read 27 November 2026.

    Under the terms of the agreement, Microsoft will acquire GitHub for $7.5 billion in Microsoft stock.

  25. 10 October 2018 Strain and stewardship

    October 2018: 60,000 patents pledged to protect Linux.

    On 10 October 2018 Microsoft joined the Open Invention Network, bringing more than 60,000 patents to a group formed to protect Linux from patent claims.

    We bring a valuable and deep portfolio of over 60,000 issued patents to OIN.

    Primary source: own-post Microsoft joins Open Invention Network to help protect Linux and open source, Microsoft Azure Blog, by The Microsoft Azure Team, published 10 October 2018. Read 27 November 2026.

  26. 26 October 2018 Strain and stewardship

    October 2018: the acquisition completes.

    Microsoft completed the GitHub acquisition on 26 October 2018.

    Microsoft has completed its acquisition of GitHub.

    Primary source: own-post Microsoft completes GitHub acquisition, The Official Microsoft Blog, published 26 October 2018. Read 27 November 2026.

    The same post says GitHub "will retain its developer-first ethos, operate independently, and remain an open platform".

  27. May 2020 Strain and stewardship

    2020: "on the wrong side of history".

    In May 2020 Brad Smith, Microsoft's president, said the company had been on the wrong side of history when open source took off, and that this applied to him personally.

    “Microsoft was on the wrong side of history when open source exploded at the beginning of the century, and I can say that about me personally,”

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft: we were wrong about open source, The Verge, by Tom Warren, published 18 May 2020. Read 27 November 2026.

    Quoted from The Verge's report of 18 May 2020, which says only that the remarks were made "in a recent MIT event". The event is usually identified as a talk hosted by MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, which this source does not say. The Verge is the record read; no recording or transcript of the talk was checked.

    Connects: Brad Smith, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

    Primary source: own-post The Windows Subsystem for Linux is now open source, Windows Developer Blog (Microsoft), by Pierre Boulay, published 19 May 2025. Read 27 November 2026.
