# Microsoft and Linux: from "a cancer" to "Microsoft loves Linux"

One company's change of position, step by step, each step from a document: the leaked memo, the speeches and interviews of 2001, the first kernel contribution, the 2014 slide, and what followed.

### 11 August 1998: An internal Microsoft memorandum of August 1998 judged open source software to be a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to the company.

Step 1. 1998: an internal memo sees a revenue and platform threat.

> OSS poses a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to Microsoft, particularly in server space.

- Source (primary, document): [Halloween Document I (Microsoft memorandum "Open Source Software: A (New?) Development Methodology", annotated)](http://catb.org/~esr/halloween/halloween1.html), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), published 1 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The memorandum (author Vinod Valloppillil, dated "Aug 11, 1998 -- v1.00" in the text) was leaked to Eric Raymond, who published it with his annotations on 1 November 1998. It is read here in Raymond's annotated copy; Microsoft has not published the original.
- Connects: Vinod Valloppillil, Eric S. Raymond, Microsoft, Halloween Document I, Leak of the Halloween Documents
- Fact id: `halloween-memo-threat-1998`

### February 2001: In February 2001 Jim Allchin, head of Windows at Microsoft, called open source an intellectual-property destroyer.

Step 2. February 2001: "an intellectual-property destroyer".

> ”Open source is an intellectual-property destroyer,” Allchin said. ”I can’t imagine something that could be worse than this for the software business and the intellectual-property business.”

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft exec calls open source a threat to innovation](https://www.cnet.com/tech/tech-industry/microsoft-exec-calls-open-source-a-threat-to-innovation/), CNET (Bloomberg News copy), published 15 February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: A Bloomberg News report as carried by CNET on 15 February 2001 ("said this week"). The news report is the record of the words; no transcript is known.
- Connects: Jim Allchin, Microsoft
- Fact id: `allchin-ip-destroyer-2001`

### 3 May 2001: On 3 May 2001 Craig Mundie of Microsoft told an audience at New York University's Stern School of Business that the "viral" GPL threatened the intellectual property of any organisation using it.

Step 3. May 2001: the GPL is called viral.

> This viral aspect of the GPL poses a threat to the intellectual property of any organization making use of it.

- Source (primary, speech): [Speech Transcript: Craig Mundie, The New York University Stern School of Business (Prepared Text of Remarks: The Commercial Software Model)](https://news.microsoft.com/speeches/speech-transcript-craig-mundie-the-new-york-university-stern-school-of-business/), Microsoft, published 3 May 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: From the prepared text of the remarks as published by Microsoft; it is not a verbatim record of the delivery.
- Connects: Craig Mundie, Microsoft, GNU General Public License, Craig Mundie's speech at NYU Stern
- Fact id: `mundie-gpl-viral-2001`

### 3 May 2001: In the same speech Mundie set out Microsoft's alternative, "Shared Source": showing source code to customers and partners while keeping the intellectual property.

Step 4. Microsoft offers "Shared Source" as its alternative.

> Shared Source is a balanced approach that allows us to share source code with customers and partners while maintaining the intellectual property needed to support a strong software business.

- Source (primary, speech): [Speech Transcript: Craig Mundie, The New York University Stern School of Business (Prepared Text of Remarks: The Commercial Software Model)](https://news.microsoft.com/speeches/speech-transcript-craig-mundie-the-new-york-university-stern-school-of-business/), Microsoft, published 3 May 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Craig Mundie, Microsoft, Craig Mundie's speech at NYU Stern
- Fact id: `mundie-shared-source-2001`

### 1 June 2001: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft's chief executive, told the Chicago Sun-Times on 1 June 2001 that Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches.

Step 5. June 2001: "a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense".

> "Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches,"

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer'](https://www.theregister.com/2001/06/02/ballmer_linux_is_a_cancer/), The Register, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The words were said in an interview with the Chicago Sun-Times published on 1 June 2001. That article could not be read: the newspaper's old address returns 404 and the Internet Archive was unreachable from this machine. The quote is taken from The Register's report of 2 June 2001, which quotes the interview. No transcript is known, so the newspaper is the record of the words and The Register is a second-hand record.
- Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview
- Fact id: `ballmer-cancer-2001`

### 2 June 2001: The remark is usually repeated in the short form "Linux is a cancer", which is the headline The Register gave its report, and which drops the qualifier about intellectual property.

Step 6. The remark is remembered in a shorter form than it was reported.

> Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer'

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer'](https://www.theregister.com/2001/06/02/ballmer_linux_is_a_cancer/), The Register, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Usually paraphrased. The short form is a headline, not the sentence Ballmer is reported to have said. The fuller sentence, as The Register quotes it, is "Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches".
- Connects: Steve Ballmer, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview
- Fact id: `ballmer-cancer-short-form`

### 5 January 2004: In January 2004 Microsoft launched "Get the Facts", an advertising campaign arguing the advantages of Windows over Linux.

Step 7. 2004: an advertising campaign against Linux.

> Microsoft Corp. on Monday launched a new advertising campaign, referred to as “Get the Facts,” which is designed to give customers information about the advantages of using its Windows operating system versus Linux, its open-source competitor.

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft Takes on Linux in Ad Campaign](https://www.eweek.com/news/microsoft-takes-on-linux-in-ad-campaign/), eWeek, published 5 January 2004. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Reported by eWeek on 5 January 2004; no Microsoft press release for the campaign was found.
- Connects: Microsoft, "Get the Facts" campaign
- Fact id: `get-the-facts-2004`

### May 2007: In May 2007 Microsoft's general counsel Brad Smith and licensing chief Horacio Gutierrez told Fortune that free and open source software infringed 235 Microsoft patents.

Step 8. 2007: the 235 patents claim.

> Revealing the precise figure for the first time, they state that FOSS infringes on no fewer than 235 Microsoft patents.

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft takes on the free world](https://fortune.com/article/microsoft-linux-intellectual-property/), Fortune, published 28 May 2007. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Disputed. The figure is often attributed to Steve Ballmer. In the Fortune article it is stated by Brad Smith and Horacio Gutierrez; Ballmer is quoted in the same article saying open source users must "play by the same rules as the rest of the business". Microsoft did not identify the patents, and the claim was never tested in court as far as the sources read here show.
- Connects: Brad Smith, Steve Ballmer, Microsoft
- Fact id: `microsoft-235-patents-2007`

- Source (primary, own-post): [Microsoft Releases Device Driver Code to the Linux Community](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/blogs/port25/microsoft-releases-device-driver-code-to-the-linux-community), Microsoft (Port 25 blog, archived on Microsoft Learn), published 20 July 2009. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Microsoft, GNU General Public License

### 20 July 2009: Microsoft's own virtualisation blog said this was the first time the company had contributed code to the Linux kernel.

- Source (primary, own-post): [Linux ICs for Hyper-V and GPLv2](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/virtualization/linux-ics-for-hyper-v-and-gplv2/381647), Microsoft (Virtualization blog, first published on TechNet), published 20 July 2009. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Microsoft
- Fact id: `microsoft-first-kernel-contribution-2009`

### 10 September 2009: In September 2009 Microsoft sponsored the new CodePlex Foundation with $1 million, to help software companies and open source communities exchange code.

Step 11. September 2009: Microsoft funds an open source foundation.

> The Foundation's mission to help the exchange of code and understanding between software companies and open source communities is really interesting to Microsoft. To help the Foundation fund its first year of operations, Microsoft is donating U.S. $1 million

- Source (primary, own-post): [The CodePlex Foundation Debuts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/blogs/port25/the-codeplex-foundation-debuts), Microsoft (Port 25 blog, archived on Microsoft Learn), published 10 September 2009. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: From Microsoft's Port 25 blog, quoting Bill Staples of Microsoft. The foundation was later renamed the Outercurve Foundation; no primary source for the renaming or for the 2006 launch of the CodePlex hosting site was fetched.
- Connects: Microsoft, CodePlex Foundation
- Fact id: `codeplex-foundation-2009`

### 20 October 2014: At a cloud event for the press in San Francisco on 20 October 2014, Satya Nadella delivered the message "Microsoft loves Linux".

Step 14. 20 October 2014: "Microsoft loves Linux".

> "Microsoft loves Linux" is generally not something one expects to hear, but that was one of the messages that Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella delivered at a San Francisco media event touting Redmond's cloud offerings on Monday.

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Redmond top man Satya Nadella: 'Microsoft LOVES Linux'](https://www.theregister.com/2014/10/20/microsoft_cloud_event/), The Register, published 21 October 2014. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The best records found are press reports by reporters at the event (The Register, PCWorld) and Microsoft's own later acknowledgement of the slide (May 2015). No Microsoft transcript or press release of 20 October 2014 containing the phrase was found.
- Connects: Satya Nadella, Microsoft, Microsoft cloud event, San Francisco
- Fact id: `microsoft-loves-linux-2014`

Step 15. The words were a slide with a heart on it.

> This was the message from Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, standing in front of an image that read “Microsoft [heart symbol] Linux,” during a Monday webcast

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft (hearts) Linux, for Azure's sake](https://www.pcworld.com/article/435928/microsoft-hearts-linux-for-azures-sake.html), PCWorld (IDG News Service), published 20 October 2014. Read 27 November 2026.

- Connects: Satya Nadella, Microsoft, Microsoft cloud event, San Francisco
- Fact id: `microsoft-loves-linux-slide-2014`

> About 20 percent of the workloads run on Azure are based on Linux, Nadella admitted.

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft (hearts) Linux, for Azure's sake](https://www.pcworld.com/article/435928/microsoft-hearts-linux-for-azures-sake.html), PCWorld (IDG News Service), published 20 October 2014. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Usually paraphrased. A reporter's summary, not a direct quotation. The Register's report says "20 per cent of Microsoft's Azure cloud is already Linux"; the two reports differ on whether the figure is workloads or the cloud as a whole.
- Connects: Satya Nadella, Microsoft, Microsoft cloud event, San Francisco
- Fact id: `azure-linux-share-2014`
