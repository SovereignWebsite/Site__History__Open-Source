The hostility

The case made against shared and open software by those who sold software, from 1976 to the lawsuits and campaigns of the 2000s, and how it ended.

  1. 3 February 1976 Shared by default

    The first statement of the argument: sharing software is stealing.

    In February 1976 Bill Gates wrote an open letter accusing computer hobbyists of stealing software, setting out the case that software should be paid for rather than shared.

    As the majority of hobbyists must be aware, most of you steal your software. Hardware must be paid for, but software is something to share. Who cares if the people who worked on it get paid?

    Primary source: document An Open Letter to Hobbyists, Wikisource (transcription of the letter), by William Henry Gates III, published 3 February 1976. Read 27 November 2026.

    Quoted from the Wikisource transcription of the letter, which is dated February 3, 1976 in the text.

    Connects: Bill Gates, An Open Letter to Hobbyists

  2. January 1994 Free software

    The Unix owner's lawsuit against BSD ends with almost all the code cleared.

    The lawsuit by Unix System Laboratories over BSD was settled in January 1994, with three files of some 18,000 removed from the Berkeley release.

    a settlement was finally reached in January 1994. The result was that three files were removed from the 18,000 that made up Networking Release 2

    Primary source: own-post Twenty Years of Berkeley Unix: From AT&T-Owned to Freely Redistributable (in Open Sources: Voices from the Open Source Revolution), O'Reilly, by Marshall Kirk McKusick, published January 1999. Read 27 November 2026.

    This is McKusick's account as a participant; no court record was fetched.

    Connects: University of California, Berkeley (Computer Systems Research Group), Unix System Laboratories v. BSDI and the University of California

    Link to this fact

  3. 11 August 1998 The label

    Microsoft's internal analysis names open source as a threat.

    An internal Microsoft memorandum of August 1998 judged open source software to be a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to the company.

    OSS poses a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to Microsoft, particularly in server space.

    Primary source: document Halloween Document I (Microsoft memorandum "Open Source Software: A (New?) Development Methodology", annotated), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Vinod Valloppillil (memorandum); Eric S. Raymond (annotations), published 1 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    The memorandum (author Vinod Valloppillil, dated "Aug 11, 1998 -- v1.00" in the text) was leaked to Eric Raymond, who published it with his annotations on 1 November 1998. It is read here in Raymond's annotated copy; Microsoft has not published the original.

    Connects: Vinod Valloppillil, Eric S. Raymond, Microsoft, Halloween Document I, Leak of the Halloween Documents

    Link to this fact

  4. 11 August 1998 The label

    The same memo says the usual tactics will not work against it.

    The same memorandum concluded that open source was credible in the long term and could not be fought with fear, uncertainty and doubt.

    OSS is long-term credible ... FUD tactics can not be used to combat it.

    Primary source: document Halloween Document I (Microsoft memorandum "Open Source Software: A (New?) Development Methodology", annotated), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Vinod Valloppillil (memorandum); Eric S. Raymond (annotations), published 1 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    Quoted from Raymond's list of "key quotes", where the ellipsis is his; the full sentence is further down in the memorandum.

    Connects: Vinod Valloppillil, Microsoft, Halloween Document I, Leak of the Halloween Documents

    Link to this fact

  5. November 1998 The label

    The memo leaks and, by Raymond's account, is acknowledged.

    According to Raymond, Microsoft admitted that the leaked memorandum was authentic within days of its publication.

    Pressed, Microsoft admitted the authenticity of Halloween I later on Monday

    Primary source: own-post Halloween Document III: Microsoft's Reaction to the "Halloween Memorandum", Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Eric S. Raymond, published 5 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    Usually paraphrased. This is Raymond's statement, written on 5 November 1998, not Microsoft's own words. The same page reproduces a response attributed to Microsoft Netherlands. No Microsoft press statement from 1998 could be fetched.

    Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Microsoft, Leak of the Halloween Documents

    Link to this fact

  6. February 2001 Hostility and adoption

    February 2001: the head of Windows calls open source an intellectual-property destroyer.

    In February 2001 Jim Allchin, head of Windows at Microsoft, called open source an intellectual-property destroyer.

    ”Open source is an intellectual-property destroyer,” Allchin said. ”I can’t imagine something that could be worse than this for the software business and the intellectual-property business.”

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft exec calls open source a threat to innovation, CNET (Bloomberg News copy), by Bloomberg News, published 15 February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    A Bloomberg News report as carried by CNET on 15 February 2001 ("said this week"). The news report is the record of the words; no transcript is known.

    Connects: Jim Allchin, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  7. February 2001 Hostility and adoption

    He wants policymakers warned.

    Allchin added that he worried about governments encouraging open source and that policymakers needed to be taught to see it as a threat.

    ”I’m an American, I believe in the American Way,” he said. ”I worry if the government encourages open source, and I don’t think we’ve done enough education of policymakers to understand the threat.”

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft exec calls open source a threat to innovation, CNET (Bloomberg News copy), by Bloomberg News, published 15 February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    Same Bloomberg News report as carried by CNET; the report is the record of the words.

    Connects: Jim Allchin, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  8. 3 May 2001 Hostility and adoption

    May 2001: a set-piece speech attacks the GPL as viral.

    On 3 May 2001 Craig Mundie of Microsoft told an audience at New York University's Stern School of Business that the "viral" GPL threatened the intellectual property of any organisation using it.

    This viral aspect of the GPL poses a threat to the intellectual property of any organization making use of it.

    Primary source: speech Speech Transcript: Craig Mundie, The New York University Stern School of Business (Prepared Text of Remarks: The Commercial Software Model), Microsoft, by Craig Mundie, published 3 May 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    From the prepared text of the remarks as published by Microsoft; it is not a verbatim record of the delivery.

    Connects: Craig Mundie, Microsoft, GNU General Public License, Craig Mundie's speech at NYU Stern

    Link to this fact

  9. 1 June 2001 Hostility and adoption

    June 2001: the chief executive calls Linux a cancer.

    Steve Ballmer, Microsoft's chief executive, told the Chicago Sun-Times on 1 June 2001 that Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches.

    "Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches,"

    Secondary source: reporting Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer', The Register, by Thomas C. Greene, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    The words were said in an interview with the Chicago Sun-Times published on 1 June 2001. That article could not be read: the newspaper's old address returns 404 and the Internet Archive was unreachable from this machine. The quote is taken from The Register's report of 2 June 2001, which quotes the interview. No transcript is known, so the newspaper is the record of the words and The Register is a second-hand record.

    Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview

    Link to this fact

  10. 1 June 2001 Hostility and adoption

    His explanation is about the licence.

    Ballmer's explanation of the remark was about licensing: he said that using any open source software obliges a company to make the rest of its software open source.

    "The way the license is written, if you use any open-source software, you have to make the rest of your software open source,"

    Secondary source: reporting Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer', The Register, by Thomas C. Greene, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.

    As quoted by The Register from the Sun-Times interview. The Register's reporter argues in the same article that this misreads the GPL.

    Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft, GNU General Public License, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview

    Link to this fact

  11. 2003 Hostility and adoption

    SCO sues IBM, claiming rights over code in Linux.

    The SCO Group's lawsuit against IBM over Unix code and Linux began in 2003.

    The case commenced in 2003, but its roots go even deeper.

    Secondary source: reporting Ancient 'Who owns Unix?' case puts a $14.25M price tag on making some claims go away, The Register, published 30 August 2021. Read 27 November 2026.

    Secondary reporting from 2021; the 2003 complaint itself was not fetched (Groklaw and the court sites refused the connection).

    Connects: The SCO Group, IBM, SCO v. IBM

    Link to this fact

  12. 5 January 2004 Hostility and adoption

    Microsoft advertises against Linux.

    In January 2004 Microsoft launched "Get the Facts", an advertising campaign arguing the advantages of Windows over Linux.

    Microsoft Corp. on Monday launched a new advertising campaign, referred to as “Get the Facts,” which is designed to give customers information about the advantages of using its Windows operating system versus Linux, its open-source competitor.

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft Takes on Linux in Ad Campaign, eWeek, by Peter Galli, published 5 January 2004. Read 27 November 2026.

    Reported by eWeek on 5 January 2004; no Microsoft press release for the campaign was found.

    Connects: Microsoft, "Get the Facts" campaign

    Link to this fact

  13. May 2007 Hostility and adoption

    Microsoft says open source infringes 235 of its patents.

    In May 2007 Microsoft's general counsel Brad Smith and licensing chief Horacio Gutierrez told Fortune that free and open source software infringed 235 Microsoft patents.

    Revealing the precise figure for the first time, they state that FOSS infringes on no fewer than 235 Microsoft patents.

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft takes on the free world, Fortune, by Roger Parloff, published 28 May 2007. Read 27 November 2026.

    Disputed. The figure is often attributed to Steve Ballmer. In the Fortune article it is stated by Brad Smith and Horacio Gutierrez; Ballmer is quoted in the same article saying open source users must "play by the same rules as the rest of the business". Microsoft did not identify the patents, and the claim was never tested in court as far as the sources read here show.

    Connects: Brad Smith, Steve Ballmer, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  14. May 2007 Hostility and adoption

    Ballmer says open source users must play by the same rules.

    In the same Fortune article Steve Ballmer said that users of free and open source software would have to play by the same rules as the rest of the business.

    FOSS patrons are going to have to “play by the same rules as the rest of the business,” he insists. “What’s fair is fair.”

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft takes on the free world, Fortune, by Roger Parloff, published 28 May 2007. Read 27 November 2026.

    Fortune is the record of the words, from its own interview; no transcript is published.

    Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  15. 9 March 2016 Mainstream

    Ballmer, retired, says the threat is behind Microsoft.

    In March 2016 Steve Ballmer said his earlier stance on Linux had been right for its time but that the threat from Linux was now "in the rearview mirror".

    Ballmer said the position was right for the time, but the threat from Linux was now "in the rearview mirror."

    Secondary source: reporting Former Microsoft CEO Ballmer does about-face on Linux technology, Reuters (as carried by The Star, Malaysia), by Reuters, published 11 March 2016. Read 27 November 2026.

    Usually paraphrased. A Reuters report, read as carried by The Star (Malaysia) because reuters.com returned 401; only the first two paragraphs were readable there. Only "in the rearview mirror" is a direct quotation; "right for the time" is the reporter's paraphrase. The remarks were made at a dinner hosted by Fortune on 9 March 2016; no transcript is known.

    Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  16. 10 October 2018 Strain and stewardship

    Microsoft acknowledges the friction over patents.

    In the same post Microsoft acknowledged past friction with the open source community over patents.

    it is no secret that there has been friction in the past between Microsoft and the open source community over the issue of patents.

    Primary source: own-post Microsoft joins Open Invention Network to help protect Linux and open source, Microsoft Azure Blog, by The Microsoft Azure Team, published 10 October 2018. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Microsoft, Open Invention Network

    Link to this fact

  17. 10 October 2018 Strain and stewardship

    It puts its patents behind Linux instead.

    On 10 October 2018 Microsoft joined the Open Invention Network, bringing more than 60,000 patents to a group formed to protect Linux from patent claims.

    We bring a valuable and deep portfolio of over 60,000 issued patents to OIN.

    Primary source: own-post Microsoft joins Open Invention Network to help protect Linux and open source, Microsoft Azure Blog, by The Microsoft Azure Team, published 10 October 2018. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Microsoft, Open Invention Network

    Link to this fact

  18. May 2020 Strain and stewardship

    The lawyer who made the 2007 patent claim says the company was wrong.

    In May 2020 Brad Smith, Microsoft's president, said the company had been on the wrong side of history when open source took off, and that this applied to him personally.

    “Microsoft was on the wrong side of history when open source exploded at the beginning of the century, and I can say that about me personally,”

    Secondary source: reporting Microsoft: we were wrong about open source, The Verge, by Tom Warren, published 18 May 2020. Read 27 November 2026.

    Quoted from The Verge's report of 18 May 2020, which says only that the remarks were made "in a recent MIT event". The event is usually identified as a talk hosted by MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, which this source does not say. The Verge is the record read; no recording or transcript of the talk was checked.

    Connects: Brad Smith, Microsoft

    Link to this fact

  19. November 2021 Strain and stewardship

    The SCO case is finally dismissed.

    In November 2021 the federal court in Utah dismissed the last of SCO v. IBM after a settlement, some eighteen years after the case began.

    All claims and counterclaims in this action, whether alleged or not alleged, pleaded or not pleaded, have been settled, compromised, and resolved in full, and are DISMISSED with prejudice and on the merits.

    Secondary source: reporting Last of original SCO v IBM Linux lawsuit settled, ZDNET, by Steven Vaughan-Nichols, published 8 November 2021. Read 27 November 2026.

    The court's order is quoted as reproduced by ZDNET (8 November 2021). The same report quotes the bankruptcy court filing giving the payment as "$14,250,000". The court documents themselves were not fetched.

    Connects: The SCO Group, IBM, SCO v. IBM
