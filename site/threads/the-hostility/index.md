# The hostility

The case made against shared and open software by those who sold software, from 1976 to the lawsuits and campaigns of the 2000s, and how it ended.

### 3 February 1976: In February 1976 Bill Gates wrote an open letter accusing computer hobbyists of stealing software, setting out the case that software should be paid for rather than shared.

Step 1. The first statement of the argument: sharing software is stealing.

> As the majority of hobbyists must be aware, most of you steal your software. Hardware must be paid for, but software is something to share. Who cares if the people who worked on it get paid?

- Source (primary, document): [An Open Letter to Hobbyists](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Open_Letter_to_Hobbyists), Wikisource (transcription of the letter), published 3 February 1976. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Quoted from the Wikisource transcription of the letter, which is dated February 3, 1976 in the text.
- Connects: Bill Gates, An Open Letter to Hobbyists
- Fact id: `gates-open-letter-1976`

### January 1994: The lawsuit by Unix System Laboratories over BSD was settled in January 1994, with three files of some 18,000 removed from the Berkeley release.

Step 2. The Unix owner's lawsuit against BSD ends with almost all the code cleared.

> a settlement was finally reached in January 1994. The result was that three files were removed from the 18,000 that made up Networking Release 2

- Source (primary, own-post): [Twenty Years of Berkeley Unix: From AT&T-Owned to Freely Redistributable (in Open Sources: Voices from the Open Source Revolution)](https://www.oreilly.com/openbook/opensources/book/kirkmck.html), O'Reilly, published January 1999. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: This is McKusick's account as a participant; no court record was fetched.
- Connects: University of California, Berkeley (Computer Systems Research Group), Unix System Laboratories v. BSDI and the University of California
- Fact id: `bsd-usl-settlement-1994`

### 11 August 1998: An internal Microsoft memorandum of August 1998 judged open source software to be a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to the company.

Step 3. Microsoft's internal analysis names open source as a threat.

> OSS poses a direct, short-term revenue and platform threat to Microsoft, particularly in server space.

- Source (primary, document): [Halloween Document I (Microsoft memorandum "Open Source Software: A (New?) Development Methodology", annotated)](http://catb.org/~esr/halloween/halloween1.html), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), published 1 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The memorandum (author Vinod Valloppillil, dated "Aug 11, 1998 -- v1.00" in the text) was leaked to Eric Raymond, who published it with his annotations on 1 November 1998. It is read here in Raymond's annotated copy; Microsoft has not published the original.
- Connects: Vinod Valloppillil, Eric S. Raymond, Microsoft, Halloween Document I, Leak of the Halloween Documents
- Fact id: `halloween-memo-threat-1998`

### 11 August 1998: The same memorandum concluded that open source was credible in the long term and could not be fought with fear, uncertainty and doubt.

Step 4. The same memo says the usual tactics will not work against it.

> OSS is long-term credible ... FUD tactics can not be used to combat it.

- Source (primary, document): [Halloween Document I (Microsoft memorandum "Open Source Software: A (New?) Development Methodology", annotated)](http://catb.org/~esr/halloween/halloween1.html), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), published 1 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Quoted from Raymond's list of "key quotes", where the ellipsis is his; the full sentence is further down in the memorandum.
- Connects: Vinod Valloppillil, Microsoft, Halloween Document I, Leak of the Halloween Documents
- Fact id: `halloween-memo-fud-1998`

### November 1998: According to Raymond, Microsoft admitted that the leaked memorandum was authentic within days of its publication.

Step 5. The memo leaks and, by Raymond's account, is acknowledged.

> Pressed, Microsoft admitted the authenticity of Halloween I later on Monday

- Source (primary, own-post): [Halloween Document III: Microsoft's Reaction to the "Halloween Memorandum"](http://catb.org/~esr/halloween/halloween3.html), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), published 5 November 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Usually paraphrased. This is Raymond's statement, written on 5 November 1998, not Microsoft's own words. The same page reproduces a response attributed to Microsoft Netherlands. No Microsoft press statement from 1998 could be fetched.
- Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Microsoft, Leak of the Halloween Documents
- Fact id: `halloween-authenticity-1998`

### February 2001: In February 2001 Jim Allchin, head of Windows at Microsoft, called open source an intellectual-property destroyer.

Step 6. February 2001: the head of Windows calls open source an intellectual-property destroyer.

> ”Open source is an intellectual-property destroyer,” Allchin said. ”I can’t imagine something that could be worse than this for the software business and the intellectual-property business.”

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft exec calls open source a threat to innovation](https://www.cnet.com/tech/tech-industry/microsoft-exec-calls-open-source-a-threat-to-innovation/), CNET (Bloomberg News copy), published 15 February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: A Bloomberg News report as carried by CNET on 15 February 2001 ("said this week"). The news report is the record of the words; no transcript is known.
- Connects: Jim Allchin, Microsoft
- Fact id: `allchin-ip-destroyer-2001`

### February 2001: Allchin added that he worried about governments encouraging open source and that policymakers needed to be taught to see it as a threat.

Step 7. He wants policymakers warned.

> ”I’m an American, I believe in the American Way,” he said. ”I worry if the government encourages open source, and I don’t think we’ve done enough education of policymakers to understand the threat.”

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft exec calls open source a threat to innovation](https://www.cnet.com/tech/tech-industry/microsoft-exec-calls-open-source-a-threat-to-innovation/), CNET (Bloomberg News copy), published 15 February 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Same Bloomberg News report as carried by CNET; the report is the record of the words.
- Connects: Jim Allchin, Microsoft
- Fact id: `allchin-american-way-2001`

### 3 May 2001: On 3 May 2001 Craig Mundie of Microsoft told an audience at New York University's Stern School of Business that the "viral" GPL threatened the intellectual property of any organisation using it.

Step 8. May 2001: a set-piece speech attacks the GPL as viral.

> This viral aspect of the GPL poses a threat to the intellectual property of any organization making use of it.

- Source (primary, speech): [Speech Transcript: Craig Mundie, The New York University Stern School of Business (Prepared Text of Remarks: The Commercial Software Model)](https://news.microsoft.com/speeches/speech-transcript-craig-mundie-the-new-york-university-stern-school-of-business/), Microsoft, published 3 May 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: From the prepared text of the remarks as published by Microsoft; it is not a verbatim record of the delivery.
- Connects: Craig Mundie, Microsoft, GNU General Public License, Craig Mundie's speech at NYU Stern
- Fact id: `mundie-gpl-viral-2001`

### 1 June 2001: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft's chief executive, told the Chicago Sun-Times on 1 June 2001 that Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches.

Step 9. June 2001: the chief executive calls Linux a cancer.

> "Linux is a cancer that attaches itself in an intellectual property sense to everything it touches,"

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer'](https://www.theregister.com/2001/06/02/ballmer_linux_is_a_cancer/), The Register, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The words were said in an interview with the Chicago Sun-Times published on 1 June 2001. That article could not be read: the newspaper's old address returns 404 and the Internet Archive was unreachable from this machine. The quote is taken from The Register's report of 2 June 2001, which quotes the interview. No transcript is known, so the newspaper is the record of the words and The Register is a second-hand record.
- Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview
- Fact id: `ballmer-cancer-2001`

### 1 June 2001: Ballmer's explanation of the remark was about licensing: he said that using any open source software obliges a company to make the rest of its software open source.

Step 10. His explanation is about the licence.

> "The way the license is written, if you use any open-source software, you have to make the rest of your software open source,"

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Ballmer: 'Linux is a cancer'](https://www.theregister.com/2001/06/02/ballmer_linux_is_a_cancer/), The Register, published 2 June 2001. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: As quoted by The Register from the Sun-Times interview. The Register's reporter argues in the same article that this misreads the GPL.
- Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft, GNU General Public License, Steve Ballmer's Chicago Sun-Times interview
- Fact id: `ballmer-cancer-licence-context-2001`

- Note: Secondary reporting from 2021; the 2003 complaint itself was not fetched (Groklaw and the court sites refused the connection).
- Connects: The SCO Group, IBM, SCO v. IBM
- Fact id: `sco-v-ibm-2003`

### 5 January 2004: In January 2004 Microsoft launched "Get the Facts", an advertising campaign arguing the advantages of Windows over Linux.

Step 12. Microsoft advertises against Linux.

> Microsoft Corp. on Monday launched a new advertising campaign, referred to as “Get the Facts,” which is designed to give customers information about the advantages of using its Windows operating system versus Linux, its open-source competitor.

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft Takes on Linux in Ad Campaign](https://www.eweek.com/news/microsoft-takes-on-linux-in-ad-campaign/), eWeek, published 5 January 2004. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Reported by eWeek on 5 January 2004; no Microsoft press release for the campaign was found.
- Connects: Microsoft, "Get the Facts" campaign
- Fact id: `get-the-facts-2004`

### May 2007: In May 2007 Microsoft's general counsel Brad Smith and licensing chief Horacio Gutierrez told Fortune that free and open source software infringed 235 Microsoft patents.

Step 13. Microsoft says open source infringes 235 of its patents.

> Revealing the precise figure for the first time, they state that FOSS infringes on no fewer than 235 Microsoft patents.

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft takes on the free world](https://fortune.com/article/microsoft-linux-intellectual-property/), Fortune, published 28 May 2007. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Disputed. The figure is often attributed to Steve Ballmer. In the Fortune article it is stated by Brad Smith and Horacio Gutierrez; Ballmer is quoted in the same article saying open source users must "play by the same rules as the rest of the business". Microsoft did not identify the patents, and the claim was never tested in court as far as the sources read here show.
- Connects: Brad Smith, Steve Ballmer, Microsoft
- Fact id: `microsoft-235-patents-2007`

### May 2007: In the same Fortune article Steve Ballmer said that users of free and open source software would have to play by the same rules as the rest of the business.

Step 14. Ballmer says open source users must play by the same rules.

> FOSS patrons are going to have to “play by the same rules as the rest of the business,” he insists. “What’s fair is fair.”

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft takes on the free world](https://fortune.com/article/microsoft-linux-intellectual-property/), Fortune, published 28 May 2007. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Fortune is the record of the words, from its own interview; no transcript is published.
- Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft
- Fact id: `ballmer-same-rules-2007`

### 9 March 2016: In March 2016 Steve Ballmer said his earlier stance on Linux had been right for its time but that the threat from Linux was now "in the rearview mirror".

Step 15. Ballmer, retired, says the threat is behind Microsoft.

> Ballmer said the position was right for the time, but the threat from Linux was now "in the rearview mirror."

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Former Microsoft CEO Ballmer does about-face on Linux technology](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2016/03/11/former-microsoft-ceo-ballmer-does-about-face-on-linux-technology), Reuters (as carried by The Star, Malaysia), published 11 March 2016. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Usually paraphrased. A Reuters report, read as carried by The Star (Malaysia) because reuters.com returned 401; only the first two paragraphs were readable there. Only "in the rearview mirror" is a direct quotation; "right for the time" is the reporter's paraphrase. The remarks were made at a dinner hosted by Fortune on 9 March 2016; no transcript is known.
- Connects: Steve Ballmer, Microsoft

### 10 October 2018: On 10 October 2018 Microsoft joined the Open Invention Network, bringing more than 60,000 patents to a group formed to protect Linux from patent claims.

Step 17. It puts its patents behind Linux instead.

> We bring a valuable and deep portfolio of over 60,000 issued patents to OIN.

### May 2020: In May 2020 Brad Smith, Microsoft's president, said the company had been on the wrong side of history when open source took off, and that this applied to him personally.

Step 18. The lawyer who made the 2007 patent claim says the company was wrong.

> “Microsoft was on the wrong side of history when open source exploded at the beginning of the century, and I can say that about me personally,”

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Microsoft: we were wrong about open source](https://www.theverge.com/2020/5/18/21262103/microsoft-open-source-linux-history-wrong-statement), The Verge, published 18 May 2020. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Quoted from The Verge's report of 18 May 2020, which says only that the remarks were made "in a recent MIT event". The event is usually identified as a talk hosted by MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, which this source does not say. The Verge is the record read; no recording or transcript of the talk was checked.
- Connects: Brad Smith, Microsoft
- Fact id: `brad-smith-wrong-side-2020`

### November 2021: In November 2021 the federal court in Utah dismissed the last of SCO v. IBM after a settlement, some eighteen years after the case began.

Step 19. The SCO case is finally dismissed.

> All claims and counterclaims in this action, whether alleged or not alleged, pleaded or not pleaded, have been settled, compromised, and resolved in full, and are DISMISSED with prejudice and on the merits.

- Source (secondary, reporting): [Last of original SCO v IBM Linux lawsuit settled](https://www.zdnet.com/article/last-of-original-sco-v-ibm-linux-lawsuit-settled/), ZDNET, published 8 November 2021. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The court's order is quoted as reproduced by ZDNET (8 November 2021). The same report quotes the bankruptcy court filing giving the payment as "$14,250,000". The court documents themselves were not fetched.
- Connects: The SCO Group, IBM, SCO v. IBM
- Fact id: `sco-ibm-settled-2021`
