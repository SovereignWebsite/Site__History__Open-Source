The label, 1998

Ten weeks, from Netscape's announcement to the summit that settled on the words "open source", followed step by step from the participants' own accounts.

  1. May 1997 Free software

    The essay that Netscape's managers read.

    Eric Raymond's essay "The Cathedral and the Bazaar", first presented in May 1997, contrasted closed "cathedral" development with the open "bazaar" style of Linux.

    I discuss these theories in terms of two fundamentally different development styles, the ``cathedral'' model of most of the commercial world versus the ``bazaar'' model of the Linux world.

    Primary source: document The Cathedral and the Bazaar (title page, revision history and abstract), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Eric S. Raymond, published May 1997. Read 27 November 2026.

    The date is from the essay's revision history, which records the first official presentation at the Linux Kongress in May 1997. The text read is version 3.0 (2000). www.catb.org did not answer; catb.org over http did.

    Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Linus Torvalds, The Cathedral and the Bazaar

  2. 22 January 1998 The label

    22 January: Netscape says it will publish its browser source.

    On 22 January 1998 Netscape announced that it would publish the source code of the next generation of its Communicator browser.

    MOUNTAIN VIEW, Calif. (January 22, 1998) — Netscape Communications Corporation (NASDAQ: NSCP) today announced bold plans to make the source code for the next generation of its highly popular Netscape Communicator client software available for free licensing on the Internet.

    Primary source: press-release January 22, 1998: the Beginning of Mozilla (reproduces the Netscape press release in full), Mitchell Baker's blog (Lizard Wrangling), by Mitchell Baker; Netscape Communications Corporation, published 22 January 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    The press release is read as reproduced in full by Mitchell Baker (later chair of Mozilla) on her blog in 2008. The same opening paragraph was forwarded to the Linux kernel mailing list on 22 January 1998. The Netscape original survives only in the Internet Archive, which could not be reached from this machine.

    Connects: Netscape Communications, Netscape source code announcement

    Link to this fact

  3. 22 January 1998 The label

    It promises a licence in the tradition of the GPL.

    Netscape said its source licence would allow modification and redistribution and would build on the heritage of the GPL.

    The company will handle free source distribution with a license which allows source code modification and redistribution and provides for free availability of source code versions, building on the heritage of the GNU Public License (GPL), familiar to developers on the Net.

    Primary source: press-release January 22, 1998: the Beginning of Mozilla (reproduces the Netscape press release in full), Mitchell Baker's blog (Lizard Wrangling), by Mitchell Baker; Netscape Communications Corporation, published 22 January 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    Same reproduction of the press release as above. The release says "GNU Public License"; the licence's name is the GNU General Public License.

    Connects: Netscape Communications, GNU General Public License, Netscape source code announcement

    Link to this fact

  4. January 1998 The label

    Netscape's chief technology officer credits Raymond, by Raymond's account.

    Raymond reports that Netscape's chief technology officer, Eric Hahn, wrote to him that his essay had been a fundamental inspiration for the decision to release the source.

    Your thinking and writings were fundamental inspirations to our decision.

    Primary source: own-post The Cathedral and the Bazaar: Epilog: Netscape Embraces the Bazaar, Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Eric S. Raymond, published February 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    Usually paraphrased. The words are from a private email as quoted by Raymond in the epilogue of his own essay; no independent copy of the email is public, so this is Raymond's record of what Hahn wrote.

    Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Netscape Communications, The Cathedral and the Bazaar

    Link to this fact

  5. February 1998 The label

    Early February: Christine Peterson thinks of "open source software".

    Christine Peterson, then executive director of the Foresight Institute, says she came up with the term "open source software" in the first week of February 1998.

    Between meetings that week, I was still focused on the need for a better name and came up with the term "open source software."

    Primary source: own-post How I coined the term 'open source', Opensource.com (Red Hat), by Christine Peterson, published 1 February 2018. Read 27 November 2026.

    Her account was written on 2 January 2006 and published on Opensource.com in February 2018.

    Connects: Christine Peterson, Foresight Institute

    Link to this fact

  6. 5 February 1998 The label

    A strategy meeting adopts it; Peterson dates it to 5 February.

    In Peterson's account the term was adopted at a strategy meeting at VA Research on 5 February 1998, where Todd Anderson put it forward and Eric Raymond endorsed it.

    Later that week, on February 5, 1998, a group was assembled at VA Research to brainstorm on strategy. [...] Todd advocated the "open source" model, and Eric endorsed this.

    Primary source: own-post How I coined the term 'open source', Opensource.com (Red Hat), by Christine Peterson, published 1 February 2018. Read 27 November 2026.

    Disputed. The date is contested between the sources. Peterson's narrative says 5 February 1998, and Raymond's call of 8 February 1998 also points to 5 February; the Open Source Initiative's history says 3 February 1998, and the editor's introduction to Peterson's own article calls 3 February the anniversary.

    Connects: Christine Peterson, Eric S. Raymond, Todd Anderson, The strategy session that adopted "open source"

    Link to this fact

  7. 3 February 1998 The label

    The OSI's history dates the same step to 3 February.

    The Open Source Initiative's own history says the "open source" label was created at a strategy session in Palo Alto on 3 February 1998, and credits Christine Peterson with suggesting it.

    The “open source” label was created at a strategy session held on February 3rd, 1998 in Palo Alto, California, shortly after the announcement of the release of the Netscape source code.

    Primary source: document History of the OSI, Open Source Initiative. Read 27 November 2026.

    Disputed. The OSI page gives 3 February 1998; Peterson's first-hand narrative gives 5 February 1998 (see open-source-meeting-peterson-account). The same OSI page says the term was "originally suggested by Christine Peterson".

    Connects: Christine Peterson, Open Source Initiative, The strategy session that adopted "open source"

    Link to this fact

  8. 8 February 1998 The label

    8 February: Raymond publishes the call to switch terms.

    On 8 February 1998 Eric Raymond publicly called on the community to replace the term "free software" with "open source", saying the group had come up with the label together.

    I brainstormed this with some Silicon Valley fans of Linux (including Larry Augustin of the Linux International board of directors) the day after my meeting with Netscape (Feb 5th). We kicked around and discarded several alternatives, and we came up with a replacement label we all liked: "open source".

    Primary source: own-post Goodbye, "free software"; hello, "open source", Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), by Eric S. Raymond, published 8 February 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    Raymond writes "we came up with"; he does not name Peterson in this passage. The date of 8 February is from his own preface to the document.

    Connects: Eric S. Raymond

    Link to this fact

  9. February 1998 The label

    Late February: Raymond and Perens found the Open Source Initiative.

    Eric Raymond and Bruce Perens founded the Open Source Initiative in late February 1998.

    OSI was jointly founded by Eric Raymond and Bruce Perens in late February 1998, with Raymond as its first president, Perens as vice-president

    Primary source: document History of the OSI, Open Source Initiative. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Bruce Perens, Open Source Initiative

    Link to this fact

  10. February 1998 The label

    Perens edits Debian's guidelines into the Open Source Definition.

    Bruce Perens says in his own account that he edited the Debian guidelines into the Open Source Definition at Raymond's suggestion.

    Raymond felt that the Debian Free Software Guidelines were the right document to define Open Source, but that they needed a more general name and the removal of Debian-specific references. I edited the Guidelines to form the Open Source Definition.

    Primary source: own-post The Open Source Definition (in Open Sources: Voices from the Open Source Revolution), O'Reilly, by Bruce Perens, published January 1999. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Bruce Perens, Eric S. Raymond, The Open Source Definition, Debian Free Software Guidelines

    Link to this fact

  11. 1998 The label

    The definition insists that open source is more than visible source code.

    The Open Source Definition opens by saying that open source is more than access to source code: the distribution terms must meet its ten criteria.

    Open source doesn’t just mean access to the source code. The distribution terms of open source software must comply with the following criteria:

    Primary source: document The Open Source Definition, Open Source Initiative. Read 27 November 2026.

    Quoted from the current text of the definition (page last modified in 2024); the definition dates from 1998.

    Connects: Open Source Initiative, The Open Source Definition

    Link to this fact

  12. 1998 The label

    The Netscape code is released and the Mozilla project begins.

    Mozilla says its project was created in 1998 with the release of the Netscape browser source code.

    The Mozilla project was created in 1998 with the release of the Netscape browser suite source code.

    Primary source: document History of the Mozilla Project, Mozilla. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Mozilla, Netscape Communications

    Link to this fact

  13. 7 April 1998 The label

    7 April: the leading developers agree on the term at O'Reilly's summit.

    At a summit organised by Tim O'Reilly in Palo Alto on 7 April 1998, leading developers agreed that "open source" or "sourceware" best described their way of working.

    the developers agreed that commercial development of the software is part of the picture, and that the terms "open source" or "sourceware" best describe the development method they support.

    Primary source: press-release Open Source Pioneers Meet in Historic Summit (O'Reilly press release, as reprinted in Linux Gazette issue 28), O'Reilly & Associates, reprinted by Linux Gazette, published 15 April 1998. Read 27 November 2026.

    The press release is read as reprinted in Linux Gazette issue 28, where it is dated April 15, 1998; O'Reilly's own copy returned 403 to this machine.

    Connects: Tim O'Reilly, O'Reilly & Associates, Freeware Summit / Open Source Summit

    Link to this fact

  14. April 1998 The label

    The meeting itself changes name, from Freeware Summit to Open Source Summit.

    The April 1998 meeting was announced as the "Freeware Summit" and within a week was being called the "Open Source Summit".

    Announced in advance as the first "Freeware Summit," by April 14 it was referred to as the first "Open Source Summit."

    Primary source: own-post How I coined the term 'open source', Opensource.com (Red Hat), by Christine Peterson, published 1 February 2018. Read 27 November 2026.

    The OSI history page calls the same meeting the "April 1998 Free Software Summit", so three names are in use for it.

    Connects: Tim O'Reilly, Christine Peterson, Freeware Summit / Open Source Summit

    Link to this fact

  15. 17 February 1999 The label

    A year on, Perens leaves the OSI.

    In the same message Perens said he would go on promoting software that fits the Open Source Definition, but independently of the Open Source Initiative.

    I'll be working to promote software that fits that definition, but independently from the Open Source Initiative.

    Primary source: mailing-list It's Time to Talk about Free Software Again (email to the editor, as published), LWN.net, by Bruce Perens, published 17 February 1999. Read 27 November 2026.

    Connects: Bruce Perens, Eric S. Raymond, Open Source Initiative, It's Time to Talk about Free Software Again
