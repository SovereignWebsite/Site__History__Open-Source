# The label, 1998

Ten weeks, from Netscape's announcement to the summit that settled on the words "open source", followed step by step from the participants' own accounts.

### May 1997: Eric Raymond's essay "The Cathedral and the Bazaar", first presented in May 1997, contrasted closed "cathedral" development with the open "bazaar" style of Linux.

Step 1. The essay that Netscape's managers read.

> I discuss these theories in terms of two fundamentally different development styles, the ``cathedral'' model of most of the commercial world versus the ``bazaar'' model of the Linux world.

- Source (primary, document): [The Cathedral and the Bazaar (title page, revision history and abstract)](http://catb.org/~esr/writings/cathedral-bazaar/cathedral-bazaar/index.html), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), published May 1997. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The date is from the essay's revision history, which records the first official presentation at the Linux Kongress in May 1997. The text read is version 3.0 (2000). www.catb.org did not answer; catb.org over http did.
- Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Linus Torvalds, The Cathedral and the Bazaar
- Fact id: `cathedral-bazaar-1997`

### 22 January 1998: On 22 January 1998 Netscape announced that it would publish the source code of the next generation of its Communicator browser.

Step 2. 22 January: Netscape says it will publish its browser source.

> MOUNTAIN VIEW, Calif. (January 22, 1998) — Netscape Communications Corporation (NASDAQ: NSCP) today announced bold plans to make the source code for the next generation of its highly popular Netscape Communicator client software available for free licensing on the Internet.

- Source (primary, press-release): [January 22, 1998: the Beginning of Mozilla (reproduces the Netscape press release in full)](https://blog.lizardwrangler.com/?p=187), Mitchell Baker's blog (Lizard Wrangling), published 22 January 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The press release is read as reproduced in full by Mitchell Baker (later chair of Mozilla) on her blog in 2008. The same opening paragraph was forwarded to the Linux kernel mailing list on 22 January 1998. The Netscape original survives only in the Internet Archive, which could not be reached from this machine.
- Connects: Netscape Communications, Netscape source code announcement
- Fact id: `netscape-announcement-1998`

### 22 January 1998: Netscape said its source licence would allow modification and redistribution and would build on the heritage of the GPL.

Step 3. It promises a licence in the tradition of the GPL.

> The company will handle free source distribution with a license which allows source code modification and redistribution and provides for free availability of source code versions, building on the heritage of the GNU Public License (GPL), familiar to developers on the Net.

- Source (primary, press-release): [January 22, 1998: the Beginning of Mozilla (reproduces the Netscape press release in full)](https://blog.lizardwrangler.com/?p=187), Mitchell Baker's blog (Lizard Wrangling), published 22 January 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Same reproduction of the press release as above. The release says "GNU Public License"; the licence's name is the GNU General Public License.
- Connects: Netscape Communications, GNU General Public License, Netscape source code announcement
- Fact id: `netscape-licence-gpl-heritage-1998`

### January 1998: Raymond reports that Netscape's chief technology officer, Eric Hahn, wrote to him that his essay had been a fundamental inspiration for the decision to release the source.

Step 4. Netscape's chief technology officer credits Raymond, by Raymond's account.

> Your thinking and writings were fundamental inspirations to our decision.

- Source (primary, own-post): [The Cathedral and the Bazaar: Epilog: Netscape Embraces the Bazaar](http://catb.org/~esr/writings/cathedral-bazaar/cathedral-bazaar/ar01s13.html), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), published February 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Usually paraphrased. The words are from a private email as quoted by Raymond in the epilogue of his own essay; no independent copy of the email is public, so this is Raymond's record of what Hahn wrote.
- Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Netscape Communications, The Cathedral and the Bazaar
- Fact id: `netscape-credits-raymond-1998`

### February 1998: Christine Peterson, then executive director of the Foresight Institute, says she came up with the term "open source software" in the first week of February 1998.

Step 5. Early February: Christine Peterson thinks of "open source software".

> Between meetings that week, I was still focused on the need for a better name and came up with the term "open source software."

- Source (primary, own-post): [How I coined the term 'open source'](https://opensource.com/article/18/2/coining-term-open-source-software), Opensource.com (Red Hat), published 1 February 2018. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Her account was written on 2 January 2006 and published on Opensource.com in February 2018.
- Connects: Christine Peterson, Foresight Institute
- Fact id: `peterson-coins-open-source-1998`

### 5 February 1998: In Peterson's account the term was adopted at a strategy meeting at VA Research on 5 February 1998, where Todd Anderson put it forward and Eric Raymond endorsed it.

Step 6. A strategy meeting adopts it; Peterson dates it to 5 February.

> Later that week, on February 5, 1998, a group was assembled at VA Research to brainstorm on strategy. [...] Todd advocated the "open source" model, and Eric endorsed this.

- Source (primary, own-post): [How I coined the term 'open source'](https://opensource.com/article/18/2/coining-term-open-source-software), Opensource.com (Red Hat), published 1 February 2018. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Disputed. The date is contested between the sources. Peterson's narrative says 5 February 1998, and Raymond's call of 8 February 1998 also points to 5 February; the Open Source Initiative's history says 3 February 1998, and the editor's introduction to Peterson's own article calls 3 February the anniversary.
- Connects: Christine Peterson, Eric S. Raymond, Todd Anderson, The strategy session that adopted "open source"
- Fact id: `open-source-meeting-peterson-account`

### 3 February 1998: The Open Source Initiative's own history says the "open source" label was created at a strategy session in Palo Alto on 3 February 1998, and credits Christine Peterson with suggesting it.

Step 7. The OSI's history dates the same step to 3 February.

> The “open source” label was created at a strategy session held on February 3rd, 1998 in Palo Alto, California, shortly after the announcement of the release of the Netscape source code.

- Source (primary, document): [History of the OSI](https://opensource.org/history), Open Source Initiative. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Disputed. The OSI page gives 3 February 1998; Peterson's first-hand narrative gives 5 February 1998 (see open-source-meeting-peterson-account). The same OSI page says the term was "originally suggested by Christine Peterson".
- Connects: Christine Peterson, Open Source Initiative, The strategy session that adopted "open source"
- Fact id: `open-source-label-osi-account`

### 8 February 1998: On 8 February 1998 Eric Raymond publicly called on the community to replace the term "free software" with "open source", saying the group had come up with the label together.

Step 8. 8 February: Raymond publishes the call to switch terms.

> I brainstormed this with some Silicon Valley fans of Linux (including Larry Augustin of the Linux International board of directors) the day after my meeting with Netscape (Feb 5th). We kicked around and discarded several alternatives, and we came up with a replacement label we all liked: "open source".

- Source (primary, own-post): [Goodbye, "free software"; hello, "open source"](http://catb.org/~esr/open-source.html), Eric S. Raymond (catb.org), published 8 February 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Raymond writes "we came up with"; he does not name Peterson in this passage. The date of 8 February is from his own preface to the document.
- Connects: Eric S. Raymond
- Fact id: `raymond-goodbye-free-software-1998`

### February 1998: Eric Raymond and Bruce Perens founded the Open Source Initiative in late February 1998.

Step 9. Late February: Raymond and Perens found the Open Source Initiative.

> OSI was jointly founded by Eric Raymond and Bruce Perens in late February 1998, with Raymond as its first president, Perens as vice-president

- Source (primary, document): [History of the OSI](https://opensource.org/history), Open Source Initiative. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Eric S. Raymond, Bruce Perens, Open Source Initiative
- Fact id: `osi-founded-1998`

### February 1998: Bruce Perens says in his own account that he edited the Debian guidelines into the Open Source Definition at Raymond's suggestion.

Step 10. Perens edits Debian's guidelines into the Open Source Definition.

> Raymond felt that the Debian Free Software Guidelines were the right document to define Open Source, but that they needed a more general name and the removal of Debian-specific references. I edited the Guidelines to form the Open Source Definition.

- Source (primary, own-post): [The Open Source Definition (in Open Sources: Voices from the Open Source Revolution)](https://www.oreilly.com/openbook/opensources/book/perens.html), O'Reilly, published January 1999. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Bruce Perens, Eric S. Raymond, The Open Source Definition, Debian Free Software Guidelines
- Fact id: `perens-edited-dfsg-into-osd`

Step 11. The definition insists that open source is more than visible source code.

> Open source doesn’t just mean access to the source code. The distribution terms of open source software must comply with the following criteria:

- Source (primary, document): [The Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd), Open Source Initiative. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: Quoted from the current text of the definition (page last modified in 2024); the definition dates from 1998.
- Connects: Open Source Initiative, The Open Source Definition
- Fact id: `osd-not-just-source`

### 1998: Mozilla says its project was created in 1998 with the release of the Netscape browser source code.

Step 12. The Netscape code is released and the Mozilla project begins.

> The Mozilla project was created in 1998 with the release of the Netscape browser suite source code.

- Source (primary, document): [History of the Mozilla Project](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/about/history/), Mozilla. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Mozilla, Netscape Communications
- Fact id: `mozilla-project-1998`

### 7 April 1998: At a summit organised by Tim O'Reilly in Palo Alto on 7 April 1998, leading developers agreed that "open source" or "sourceware" best described their way of working.

Step 13. 7 April: the leading developers agree on the term at O'Reilly's summit.

> the developers agreed that commercial development of the software is part of the picture, and that the terms "open source" or "sourceware" best describe the development method they support.

- Source (primary, press-release): [Open Source Pioneers Meet in Historic Summit (O'Reilly press release, as reprinted in Linux Gazette issue 28)](https://linuxgazette.net/issue28/oreilly.html), O'Reilly & Associates, reprinted by Linux Gazette, published 15 April 1998. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The press release is read as reprinted in Linux Gazette issue 28, where it is dated April 15, 1998; O'Reilly's own copy returned 403 to this machine.
- Connects: Tim O'Reilly, O'Reilly & Associates, Freeware Summit / Open Source Summit
- Fact id: `open-source-summit-1998`

### April 1998: The April 1998 meeting was announced as the "Freeware Summit" and within a week was being called the "Open Source Summit".

Step 14. The meeting itself changes name, from Freeware Summit to Open Source Summit.

> Announced in advance as the first "Freeware Summit," by April 14 it was referred to as the first "Open Source Summit."

- Source (primary, own-post): [How I coined the term 'open source'](https://opensource.com/article/18/2/coining-term-open-source-software), Opensource.com (Red Hat), published 1 February 2018. Read 27 November 2026.
- Note: The OSI history page calls the same meeting the "April 1998 Free Software Summit", so three names are in use for it.
- Connects: Tim O'Reilly, Christine Peterson, Freeware Summit / Open Source Summit
- Fact id: `freeware-summit-renamed-1998`

### 17 February 1999: In the same message Perens said he would go on promoting software that fits the Open Source Definition, but independently of the Open Source Initiative.

Step 15. A year on, Perens leaves the OSI.

> I'll be working to promote software that fits that definition, but independently from the Open Source Initiative.

- Source (primary, mailing-list): [It's Time to Talk about Free Software Again (email to the editor, as published)](https://static.lwn.net/1999/0218/a/bp.html), LWN.net, published 17 February 1999. Read 27 November 2026.
- Connects: Bruce Perens, Eric S. Raymond, Open Source Initiative, It's Time to Talk about Free Software Again
- Fact id: `perens-leaves-osi-1999`
